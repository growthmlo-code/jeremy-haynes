# Content & Ad Creative Strategy — Jeremy Haynes

> Built verbatim from five Jeremy Haynes YouTube transcripts (013, 029, 043, 048, 050). Everything below is grounded in those transcripts. Short quotes carry a [Video Title] citation. Nothing has been added from outside knowledge.

Jeremy's whole channel exists to "crack million-dollar months, whether it's the first million a month or the next million a month." His content and ad-creative systems all serve that one goal, and he explicitly targets people already doing "a couple hundred grand a month, if not a couple million bucks a month."

---

## PART A — ORGANIC CONTENT STRATEGY

### 1. The core thesis: optimize around revenue, not views

Jeremy's governing principle flips the usual organic playbook:

> "I never optimize around views. I optimize around revenue." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

Every video is made from the perspective of what his *highest-level* ideal customer wants to know — not the lowest-level viewer. Ideas come straight from his actual clients and customers (patterns he spots across one-on-one calls, group chats, and DMs). Example given: when "everybody's CPMs just started to shoot through the roof," he saw the pattern across DMs/group chat/calls and made content on it.

He speaks exclusively to one level of person:

> "My content is specifically for rich people that are trying to get a hell of a lot richer." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

He is fine with low view counts because the views are qualified. Typical main-channel videos run ~2,000–6,000 views, outliers 10,000+, "bangers" ~20,000 — "very qualified views."

### 2. YouTube is the engine; everything else is redistribution

> "The main driver of actual revenue comes from YouTube. YouTube is the number one nurture engine and discovery channel." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

He quantifies the imbalance with what he calls the **90/10 system** (a sharper version of 80/20): YouTube cost only ~$3.5K–4K/mo yet drove "90 plus percent... I'd say like 95%" of people who show up to buy or recognize him. By contrast the other channels cost far more (blog $5K/mo, IG team $8K/mo, LinkedIn+Twitter $6.3K/mo) and contributed "like 10% of the impact."

> "I've never had a single person in real life walk up to me... and said anything about emails... never anything about blog... It's YouTube." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

### 3. The multi-channel architecture (each channel has ONE purpose)

The lesson he learned from a Roblox streamer at his gym with 7 channels: **the algorithm distributes content to the people most likely to watch that content type**, so segment by intent across separate channels.

- **Main channel** — all business lessons. Three drops/week:
  1. **Monday = talking-head** (instructor explaining an in-depth topic; this is where most call-to-actions live because it's just him talking). Framed as **bottom-of-funnel**.
  2. **Wednesday = business breakdown** (interview/demonstration content — shows lessons in application; covers many lessons but shallower on each). Framed as **mid-funnel**.
  3. **Saturday = testing/discovery slot** — the one slot where he tries to grow total views; **top-of-funnel**.
- **Second channel ("Lessons for my kids")** — mindset content, posted Sundays. Mindset-seekers "are typically not as financially qualified," so he keeps them off the main channel but can funnel them toward it.
- **Third channel ("The Haynes feed")** — clips, shorts, and 2–9 minute "mids," optimized around search. Posts ~6×/day, up to ~15 on aggressive days.

A hard rule on shorts:

> "Poor people watch shorts. Rich people do not watch shorts. I will not let my main channel algorithm become polluted and poisoned by starting to post shorts on it." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

### 4. The "fractionalize" conversion model (for an education/info business)

Because he sells education products, he deliberately withholds:

> "I fractionalize the knowledge... I'm talking about 10 to 20% of what I actually could be talking to you about. I save all the paid stuff, the juicy stuff for my paid offers." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

Crucial caveat — the model depends on your offer type:
- **Education/info offer → the 10–20% fractional approach** (withhold the how-to, sell the rest).
- **Done-for-you / agency service → reveal essentially everything**, make it sound hard/in-depth, and change the CTA to "if you want it easier, have us do it for you."

### 5. Always make call-to-actions

He breaks with organic "gurus" who avoid CTAs to accumulate views:

> "I make tons of call to actions... I deeply believe in the value of my paid offers." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

CTAs are woven in naturally, never abruptly. He considers it an "ethical responsibility" to convert viewers because they'll take paid material more seriously and benefit more.

### 6. Treat YouTube like a TV network (consistency as the moat)

> "We will never miss a drop... I'm filming by candle light. If the power went out, the videos are getting filmed." [My Entire Organic Content Strategy]

YouTube is "a major moat" — it takes a long time to become a distribution platform. Early on, views are low and demoralizing, **but the few views you do get are people actively in the sales process** — your highest-quality viewers and initial subscriber base.

### 7. Redistribution system (efficiency play)

Original setup: 2 iPads (one filming 4K/60fps, one to draw on), tripod + sandbag, Rode mics. Only ~2 hrs/week filming for the first two years (8 hrs/month). An outside team (UK, ~$3.5K–4K/mo, pay-per-video) handled editing, thumbnails, posting, descriptions, chapters — **no ideation** (ideas always come from Jeremy/his clients).

YouTube videos are the source; everything else is repurposed in his voice:
- **X/Twitter + LinkedIn** (agency "The Birdhouse") → threads, tweets, blogs, newsletters.
- **Instagram** → clips (old approach), now a bigger operation (see below).
- **Blog (jeremyhaynes.com)** → AI-search-engine optimization, to be cited as the top expert.
- **Email** → an AI agent sends personalized emails pointing to free stuff, videos, newsletters, blogs, lead magnets.

**The redistribution channels' job is to push people BACK to YouTube.** "Every call to action... go back and watch this video... on YouTube."

### 8. Modern Instagram approach (the one channel upgraded from pure redistribution)

He historically avoided scaling on IG (calls it the "number one sketchy platform to scale your business" — channels can be taken down or DMs restricted via payoffs). Now that he has recurring-revenue cushion, he takes more risk. Current IG system:

- **5 posts/day**: 3 are reels he personally filmed (films ~15 reels in one 1–2 hr session/week, with in-house IG guy Alex and templated hooks), 2 are images (usually carousels — e.g. screenshotted tweet threads, or screenshots of his video captions teaching a lesson).
- **Every single post has a "comment CTA"** — "comment this to get this" (free lead magnet/SOP/video, OR paid offer like Jeremy AI, Inner Circle, a masterclass). There is never a post without it.
- At least 1 of the 5 daily posts promotes Jeremy AI (≥5 Jeremy AI promos/week).
- Reels are hard for him because he must compress a lesson into "a hook, a simple body, and then a CTA" under 1–2 minutes.

Current total filming time: ~12 hrs/week on YouTube (2 days, ~6 hrs each) + 1–2 hrs reels ≈ 13–14 hrs/week (48 hrs/month vs. the old 8 hrs/month) — justified because organic is now a huge revenue driver.

## PART B — PAID AD CREATIVE STRATEGY

### 1. The organic → warm → cold progression (and where people get stuck)

Jeremy frames paid as a waterfall that most people never complete:

1. **Organic** — where you master hooks, nurture sequences, and the path from unaware → aware → nurtured → sold → upsold. Most people get stuck here and never go paid.
2. **Warm paid** — "glorified retargeting" of the audience you built organically. Effective but ceiling-limited by audience size. (A warm audience = video-viewers [prefer the **25% view threshold** and up, not the weak 3-second], plus IG/FB/TikTok/LinkedIn/YouTube page engagement — profile visits, saves, post interaction, messages. Page/profile interaction is "typically of higher value than the video views.")
3. **Cold paid** — strangers who don't know you. "Cold paid advertising is not hard... it is challenging but it's not hard. Most people just don't get to this step."

### 2. THE key difference that makes cold paid profitable at scale: condense the organic timeline + close the nurture gap

This is the central lesson of video 029. The whole game is to **replicate the organic sales process via paid ads, then condense its timeline** — because with paid you're fronting cash and must recoup it within the credit-card billing cycle.

**Be self-aware of the customer path.** Organically, buyers show up hot because they consumed a key video (or a set of key videos) over a long window. You must identify those key moments and either condense one long asset or break up the set into a paid sequence.

The condensed front-end sequence:
1. **Direct-response ad** — condense the key message to **under 90 seconds**: "here's what I do → here's why it makes sense for you → handle objections → CTA."
2. **The page** — a call scheduler, a webinar, or DM ads (the "big three"). On it, a **VSL** (or the DM conversation doing the VSL's job).
   - VSL length: **10–15 minutes MAX**, ideally **2–5 minutes**. 2–5 min VSLs retain far better (can still net ~50% watching at the end); 10–15 min VSLs drop off steeply in the first 2 minutes but the survivors are more sold.
   - VSL flow: what we do → why it makes sense → the offer → objections → testimonials → reiterate offer → more objections/testimonials → final CTA.
3. **THE MISSING STEP — the framing window / "Hammer Cycle"** between the booking and the call. This is where almost everyone fails:

> "They miss a tremendous gap right here between that VSL page and the conversation." [This ONE Difference Makes Cold Paid Ads Profitable]

**Interest level is dynamic, not static.** Jeremy's interest ladder (low → high): *eh/need more info → curious → general interest → high conviction → sold.* Paid leads book calls at merely "curious" — **not because they're ready to buy, but because they have more questions.** Organic leads arrive "sold" because ~18 questions/touchpoints got answered over weeks; paid leads have only had 2–10 answered.

> "If you want a lead who books a call to show up ready to buy... we have to answer all the questions that come up in between booking the call and the conversation." [This ONE Difference Makes Cold Paid Ads Profitable]

A poor show rate is diagnosed as failure to nurture in the framing window. He illustrates with a personal Dubai-tax-ad story: strong hook got him to book, but 9 days of only "your call is coming up" reminder emails let him research his way *out* of being sold, and he didn't even show.

### 3. The "Hammer Them" campaign (back-end content nurture)

The mechanics of filling the framing window:
- **6 emails per day** (minimum) between booking and the call — "that's one piece of how we're going to hammer people." Content categories: questions, questions-that-arise-from-questions, expectations (e.g. timeline to result), objections, money/financing. Pull these from your sales team ("what are the most common things people ask on a call?").
- **Up to 50 pieces of content in front of them inside ~72 hours** via a retargeting audience:
  - Build a custom audience of people who took the signal action (booked a call / DM'd / etc.).
  - **Minimum ~100 people** needed to fire ads. High volume → tighten the window to the actual book-to-call gap; low volume → widen it (e.g. a 30-day window) to capture 100 people.
  - Works on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, even Snapchat.
  - **Judge it by frequency** — get the frequency as high as possible in the ideal window (if you have 20 pieces, aim for frequency 20). Use a **3-second view exclusion** so once someone sees a video, they never see it again and cycle to the next one.
  - Optional advanced split: one campaign to aid show rate (before the call) + a second campaign after the call to aid the sales process.

Why it works — his "propagandist" framing: people inherit the content they consume as "data to think with," then regurgitate it as their own thoughts. Put the right data in (e.g. a piece showing the math of why financing makes sense) and by the time they're on the call they already think your way.

> "If we can put the right data into the mind of a lead before a call... we will successfully... replicate the organic sales process via paid ads... We turn our salespeople into cashiers." [This ONE Difference Makes Cold Paid Ads Profitable]

The nurture window is what amplifies ROAS (it lifts show rate, close rate, and shortens the sales cycle). Also train salespeople to recognize whether a lead is organic / warm / cold — don't treat them all the same.

## PART C — CREATIVE TESTING METHODOLOGY (Post-Andromeda, 2026)

From video 043. Meta's Andromeda update invalidated old best practices. Andromeda rollout timeline cited: isolated testing Nov–Dec 2024 → ~a handful of accounts April 2025 → ~half of accounts by July 2025. If you're not updated, "you're getting absolutely cooked."

### 1. Campaign structure

- **Two campaign types**: a cold campaign (the focus) and a warm campaign (barely funded unless the warm audience is sizable).
- **Cold campaign = 3 ad sets**: **Broad**, **Interest stack**, **Lookalike stack**.
  - "Stack" = load tons of interests/behaviors/demographics into ONE ad set — do not separate them. "Targeting is controlled by messaging," not at the ad-set level anymore (that's an archaic belief).
  - Lookalike stack = a bunch of 1% lookalikes (highest-value customers, customers, leads who showed up to calls, qualified calls booked, etc.).
- **Use ABO (ad-set budgets), not CBO**, so you can control distribution across the three ad sets.
- **All three ad sets contain the SAME 25–30 unique ads.**
- Plus **5 body-copy variations and 5 headlines** — the *same* five across every ad (written broad enough to apply to all).
- Turn **OFF** all AI "Advantage creative" settings and site links. The only Advantage feature he likes is the one that **highlights a positive comment**.

### 2. Messaging diversification (the Andromeda best practice)

> "We will create 25 to 30 individual reasons that somebody should go and convert... We will literally film 25 to 30 total unique through and through creatives." [The BEST Ad Creative Testing Strategy for 2026]

Do NOT just swap a hook onto the same body+CTA — each of the 25–30 is a fully unique creative with its own reason to convert.

### 3. Reading winners — kill the "every creative deserves distribution" belief

> "Outdated belief is every creative deserves distribution. It doesn't." [The BEST Ad Creative Testing Strategy for 2026]

- Out of 25–30 ads, **only 1–3 will get essentially all the reach/distribution**; the other 27–29 spend pennies. **This is correct and desired** — the trillion-dollar algorithm is choosing the ones most likely to convert.
- The archaic reaction (duplicating one ad set 25–30 times with one ad each to force distribution) causes **artificially inflated cost per result** and excessive audience overlap, and Meta still rewards account simplification. Don't do it.
- A "winner" ≠ just a positive ROAS on $100 spend. A true winner: **positive ROAS + consistent over a relatively broad period + able to absorb serious ad spend.** Usually only a **single-digit percentage** of creatives ever hit this — which is exactly why volume matters.

### 4. The scaling loop ("if this, then that")

1. Launch 25–30 unique ads across the 3 ad sets → get 1–3 winners.
2. **Scale** the winning ad set until it hits a **ceiling** (above it, cost/result climbs and volume drops). Push spend back down to the ceiling — "the ceiling becomes the key." Convert it to a **foundational campaign/ad set**.
3. **Duplicate** that ad set (same targeting). In the duplicate: **keep the ~27–29 non-reach ads, REMOVE the 1–3 winners, add 1–3 new ads** → back to 25–30. Drop this duplicate to a **test budget** and repeat.
4. **Quality gate on winners:** if the new 1–3 winners bring a good cost-per-result but your sales team / webinar / call feedback says "these people suck," label them **losers** even though they technically won, avoid that messaging, kill that ad set, duplicate, and relaunch with new ads.

### 5. Why keeping unused ads around is good

Having 27–29 ads "on the side" is a feature, not wasted effort: relaunch the ad set to overcome ad fatigue, or swap in replacements fast when you need to duplicate — so you don't have to re-film from scratch (he notes most people are "intolerably" slow to film/edit new ads).

## PART D — AD CREATIVE FORMATS RANKED (Worst → Best)

From video 048. Ranking criterion: **what has actually helped organizations crack million-dollar months — specifically, what can absorb serious spend and stay stable at scale.** (A format can have a great ROAS but rank low if it can't scale or you can't churn out volume of it.)

### S-Tier (best — scale these hard)
- **Static reels** — B-roll video(s) running behind text overlays (e.g. 10 clips, cuts every few seconds, text on top). "The consistent undeniable winners." Easy to churn out at volume. Example cited: Inner Circle member Brooke runs lifestyle-flex B-roll with Star-Wars-style scrolling text for an e-com biz-op. Make a point with text, change to the next point.
- **Talking head** — "an OG ad creative format that works so well." Film under 2 min (ideally 30–60 sec), add jump cuts + captions, works no matter the funnel. Easy to plug a script into and produce at volume. **Caveat:** requires star power/charisma — "you got to have the power of the tongue." Fails if you're bad on camera.
- **AI-generated creatives** *(so close to S it's effectively listed here with a caveat)* — broad category (AI UGC, Google VEO video, Higgsfield movie-trailer-style ads that used to cost hundreds of thousands). "Really close to being rated in S-tier... undeniably could be in the S-tier if you've got your [stuff] together." Held back only because most people can't prompt the tools well enough — the prompting gap keeps the general case at A.
- **Carousels** *(arguably S when exploited for creative testing)* — see A-tier.

### A-Tier
- **Static images** — "undeniably A tier." Great at scale, maintain good CPMs as spend rises, very stable. **Context caveat:** excellent for **webinar opt-ins**; on a **VSL call funnel** for high-ticket they bring leads through *less educated* (people don't consume the VSL/front-end), so you need strong back-end selling systems. This circumstantial downside keeps them out of S.
- **Carousels** — "a huge fan." Variants: testimonial carousels, feature-based, combined pain/objection/feature/testimonial, and the proven **Twitter/tweet-thread carousel** (screenshot each tweet in a thread → one carousel image each — "unbelievable how well it works"). Underutilized; needs creative thinking. Arguably S-tier when individually created for dedicated creative testing.

### B-Tier
- **Content ads (running your winning organic post as a paid ad)** — makes sense to test; organic often works because it lacks the "sponsored/ad" label. But "we just don't see it absorb a lot of spend," so B.
- **UGC** (real paid humans, not AI) — can work, but for high-ticket product/service "they don't ever really sound that genuine" — the biggest drawback. Costs more than AI UGC. Higher if you find someone genuinely convincing.
- **Green screen organic** — the floating-head-over-background format (TikTok/Reels style). Works, but most people don't produce it at serious volume, so few winners surface.
- **Testimonial ads** (ranked B, floats A↔B) — great testimonials punchy/short/direct (combined with carousels could be S); long, un-cut, caption-less testimonial videos could be D. Mostly a **bottom-of-funnel** creative that does well at small scale but can't absorb big spend, hence B.

### C-Tier
- **VSL run as an ad** — "when it works, it works great," but not consistently. A **2–5 min** VSL as an ad is actually A-tier; most real VSLs are 10–20 min, which lands B/C mainly due to length.
- **Podcasts run as ads** — same logic as VSLs but "generally always way longer," so C. **Pro tip:** cut key podcast moments and remove the fluff — stacking several "aha moments / epiphanies" back-to-back-to-back raises effectiveness and the ranking a lot. They rank low mainly because people just "download the thing and plug it into the ads manager."
- **Meme ads** — depends on creativity and alignment to your niche. Can drive one of the largest link click-through rates when it hits on genuine industry humor, but generates **curiosity clicks, not high intent**, so C.
- **Retargeting ads** — warm/bottom-of-funnel (e.g. objection-handler video after they click). **High ROAS but little total money** because you can't put much spend behind it. Not top-of-funnel, so C.

### D / F-Tier
- Over a decade-plus career Jeremy says there's almost nothing he'd put in D/F. The one exception:
  > "If I were to rank something inside of the F-tier or the D-tier category, I would rank completely unedited ads." [I Ranked Every Ad Creative Format From Worst to Best]

### The volume principle underpinning the whole ranking
> "Volume matters so much when you are testing ad creatives because most people never actually run their hit rate." [I Ranked Every Ad Creative Format From Worst to Best]

Because only a single-digit percentage of creatives ever truly win at scale, **favor formats you can churn out in high volume** (static reels, talking head, images, carousels) over formats you can't (full VSLs/podcasts as ads).

## PART E — THE CONTENT AD STRATEGY ("The Forester")

From video 050. This is Jeremy's named system for making **cold traffic buy at scale** by weaving true content into paid campaigns.

### Background: content-first ad strategies
Nearly a decade ago he discovered the power of spending big to distribute *content* (not just DR ads) to future buyers — "content-first ad strategies," replicating the organic sales process via paid. Lineage:
- **The Venus Flytrap (original)** — a forced *sequence*: watch a threshold of video 1 → unlock video 2 → threshold of video 2 → video 3 → threshold of video 3 → unlock direct-response ads. High friction, fewer people reached DR, but produced very warm leads. (A "Venus Flytrap 2.0" has its own video.)
- **The Hammer Them campaign** — back-end: swarm someone who booked/opted-in/bought low-ticket with dozens of short + long content pieces at a target frequency. (See Part C above.)
- **The Forester** — today's featured front-end strategy.

### When you need content ads (and when you don't)
> "The only times that you don't... need... content ads is when you have an absolute monster of a sales team." [The Content Ad Strategy That Makes Cold Traffic Buy at Scale]

If your team can take an ice-cold lead-form/opt-in/low-ticket buyer straight to high-ticket, skip it. If your team does its best work when leads are warmed up (consumed content, VSLs, webinars first) — "you're one of the majority of businesses on Earth" — content ads will help.

### What "content" means here
Literal content — things you'd post to IG/FB/TikTok: **"No call to actions, no button links, just literal content."** Not DR ads.

### The mechanism: the "content cycle bin"
A **content cycle bin** = one campaign, **one ad set per piece of content**, each ad set running a **3-second video-view exclusion** so a viewer never sees the same piece twice (as they scroll, the next piece hits them). Load dozens (or hundreds, account-spend permitting) of unique pieces. Runs on FB, IG, TikTok, LinkedIn (not recommended on YouTube — best on scroll/suggestion feeds).

### The Forester — three steps, two running concurrently

**Step 1 — Content cycle bin #1 (the ONLY job: make cold people retargetable cheaply).**
- Target the **same ice-cold audiences** you'd otherwise hit with cold DR (broad, lookalikes, interest stacks).
- Goal is only a **3-second view** of ANY piece. Pick content that will simply get watched — nothing else matters here.
- Cost to get someone retargetable is tiny — "about 1 to 2 pennies," sometimes a tenth or hundredth of a penny.
- Why: social platforms charge **less to reach people who've already engaged with you**. By manufacturing a 3-second engagement, you lower the CPM for the DR ads that follow.
> "I'm going to manufacture that by doing the cheapest possible thing I can possibly spend money on to get somebody retargetable." [The Content Ad Strategy That Makes Cold Traffic Buy at Scale]
- Build an audience of "viewed ≥3 sec of any video in bin #1" → that's your retargeting pool for Step 2.

**Step 2 — Direct-response ads (to the 3-sec viewers).**
- Business as usual: any creative, any funnel — call funnel, DM ads, webinar, low-ticket, lead forms, quiz funnel. Same DR you'd normally run, now hitting warmed, cheaper-to-reach people.

**Step 3 — Content cycle bin #2 (runs CONCURRENTLY with Step 2, but strategic).**
- Unlike bin #1 (which just wants a view), bin #2's content is **deliberately selected** for someone being hit with DR at the same time: product/service-awareness content, testimonials, use-case content, unboxing, onboarding/first-experience videos, long-form expertise videos (like the one being watched), short-form versions, stage talks, podcasts.
- Same 3-second-exclusion logic; same audience (the 3-sec viewers).

### The "swarm effect" and why it works
Running content everywhere alongside DR creates a **strong familiarity bias** — you appear all over the newsfeed, one of the few advertisers actually spending on content.
- Results seen: CPMs cut dramatically — "we've seen CPMs go from an audacious like two to three hundred dollars down to dozens of dollars," sometimes chopped in half, reductions "upwards of sometimes hundreds of percent."
- Outcomes: warmer, more aware leads → better show rates, connection rates, close rates, shorter sales cycles, higher AOV, more pips (people confident enough to do a deal).
- **ROI is hard to attribute directly** — blend the content spend with the DR spend to justify it, and **split test** by running DR-only on its own vs. a Forester setup against it.
