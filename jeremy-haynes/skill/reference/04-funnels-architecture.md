# 04 · Funnels Architecture — Jeremy Haynes' Real Funnel Systems

> Built strictly from Jeremy Haynes' own YouTube transcripts (video IDs in **Source videos** at the end). Every claim, number, and quote below is grounded in those transcripts. Nothing has been added from outside knowledge.

Jeremy Haynes is an agency owner (Megalodon Marketing) who, per his own framing, has helped "41 different businesses" scale to "million-dollar months" (he references working on #42 and #43). His flagship funnel is the **call funnel** for high-ticket products/services. This document captures his funnel architectures, benchmarks, and best practices.

---

## 0. The master rule: funnel choice follows your sales team

> "Your funnel choice is determined by the strengths and weaknesses of your sales team." [Every $1M/Month Funnel Explained In 42 Mins]

- If your strength is **organic content**, your closers are likely "layup specialists" / "cashier at a grocery store" — they close warm deals but cannot run education calls. Rolling an organic call funnel onto cold paid traffic will "face-fuck you with reality." [Every $1M/Month Funnel Explained]
- **Webinars** create an organic-content-like (pre-educated/warm) buyer, condensing the sales cycle — good for weaker sales teams.
- **Call funnels on paid** require a dialed-in team that can do education calls; otherwise it's "a terrible funnel."
- Always ask: can my team handle the **testing bandwidth** each funnel demands?

## 1. The $1M/Month Call Funnel — full anatomy

Jeremy's core definition and flow, assembled from all five call-funnel videos:

> "I define a call funnel as we are trying to get a[n] application filled out to qualify somebody. The qualified person then is allowed to schedule a booked call." [The ULTIMATE Funnel For Selling High Ticket Services]

### The step list (ad → close → follow-up)

1. **Direct-response ad** (talking-head works great) → clicks to the funnel page. No opt-in.
2. **Funnel page** = Headline + VSL (or mini-webinar) + **application with scheduler embedded**.
3. **Application (Typeform)** with conditional logic → qualified branch routes straight into an **embedded Calendly scheduler**; unqualified branch → unqualified/drop-sell page.
4. **Qualified → books call** → lands on a **Confirmation page** (the start of "back-end selling systems").
5. **Back-end selling systems fire** between booking and call (confirmation-page videos, value-dense emails, Hammer Them retargeting, setter pre-call contact, AI-manipulation).
6. **Setter/closer manual pre-call contact** (iPhone text + selfie video).
7. **Show up → closer runs the call** (1-call or 2-call close).
8. **Post-call follow-up / cleanup** by setters/cleaners; CRM pipeline stages updated.
9. **Pixel conditioning loop**: only qualified scheduled calls fire back to the ad platform.

> "That process paired with a high ticket solid offer [is] one of the best most predictable and reliable ways to hit a million dollars a month. It's simple math." [The ULTIMATE Funnel]

### Non-negotiable prerequisite: a great offer

> "It all starts with a great offer. If you've got a [bad] offer just stop watching this video and just go make yourself a great offer." [The ULTIMATE Funnel]

A great offer drops every cost (cost per call, cost per qualified call, cost per show) and lifts show rate, close rate, and shortens the sales cycle. A bad offer inflates all of them — and people often misdiagnose this as "my marketer sucks / my sales team sucks" when the real problem is the offer (something people *want*, not *need*; have to be convinced).

### High-ticket price floor

- High ticket = **above $5K**. "$5,000 is generally the lowest." Sometimes you can get away with **$3,500**, but he prefers **5K minimums**. No cap on the top end. [The ULTIMATE Funnel]
- Example of the ceiling: a capital-raise client requires accredited investors with **$1M minimum** to even talk; they get **46% of leads** to come through with **$5M+**. [The ULTIMATE Funnel]

## 2. The funnel page (front end)

### Keep it simple — three components

> "They are still as simple as what we've historically ran them. We do a headline, we do a video, and we do an application." [ONLY Guide to Build Wildly Profitable VSL Call Funnels]

### No opt-in (critical)

- Running without opt-ins is the default. Opt-ins add friction and inflate cost per call.
- Real example: "Andres" ran a call funnel with an opt-in getting a **2.62% opt-in rate** → **$520 cost per call** — "willfully, artificially inflating the cost per call." [How To Scale A Call Funnel]
- **The opt-in justification test**: pull a CRM tag report of people who opted in but did NOT apply/schedule — how much did your setters close from them in the last 30/60/90 days? If it's a worthwhile amount, keep it. "In almost every other instance... an opt-in is not justified." [How To Scale / 40-50 Qualified Calls]
- For **rich** audiences: never use an opt-in. "Rich people don't give a [damn] to opt in for your pitch. It's a pitch." They want low commitment to see if it's right for them. [Call Funnels That Make Rich People Buy]
- Instead of capturing emails via opt-in, Jeremy textually remarkets to page visitors who didn't apply ("pocket opportunity"): he reaches **~50%** of visitors (those retargetable after pixel-blockers) vs. an opt-in page that captures less than half and then only reaches a fraction of them by email open rate.

### The VSL / mini-webinar options

Front-end video can be any of:
- **Traditional VSL** — standing in front of camera, articulating. Not everyone is good at this.
- **Mini-webinar (1.0)** — structured, templated, scripted screen-recorded presentation (~5–15 min). Less daunting; no need to show your face. Good for products **under $5,000** or any high-ticket. [40-50 Qualified Calls / The ULTIMATE Funnel]
- **Mini-webinar 2.0** — for **make-money offers above $5,000** (use even under $5K if it's a make-money offer). More sophisticated. He only teaches this inside paid offers. [40-50 Qualified Calls]
- **Podcast embed** — client "Andrew" (getting a $1M/mo trophy) embedded a plain 1-hour Zoom podcast (laptop webcam) as the VSL for a real-estate info offer; skippable; consistently brings qualified consumers. [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

### Front end = "scanner mode," back end = "justification mode"

This is a core mental model:
> "On the front end... they're in a mode we call scanner mode where... they're trying to be very energy conservative... they want to quickly determine, is this for me?" After booking, "we see a switch... they go from scanner mode to justification mode." [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

Implication: **most VSL consumption happens AFTER the call is booked, not before.** Trying to force front-end consumption just raises cost. "People gaslight themselves on call funnels that the consumption happens on the front end. It doesn't." [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

- **Play rate**: % of page visitors who press play. Jeremy has seen single digits to 90th-percentile depending on niche. [Every $1M/Month Funnel]
- **Engagement rate**: average % of the video consumed (e.g., 50% of a 10-min VSL = 5 min watched). [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

### VSL best practices

- **VSL chapters** (like YouTube chapters) — the single biggest recent win. Credited to inner-circle member **Daniel Fazio** (Q1 2025 mastermind talk). Immediately rolled out to all clients, no split test. Lets people scrub to the parts "for them." Increased retention; people come through more qualified. [ONLY Guide / Call Funnels for Rich People]
  - Quantified lift: **engagement rate jumped 37%** by adding chapter titles. [Call Funnels That Make Rich People Buy]
- **Show the player controls** and allow **playback speed up to 2x** (0.25 increments). Rich people love control; a 40-min VSL at 2x is effectively ~20 min, and ~10–15 min of real exposure after skipping. [Call Funnels That Make Rich People Buy]
- **Don't force consumption.** Only 1 of ~220 inner-circle members (named "Marcel") successfully forces consumption via a **45-minute delay before the application appears**; everyone else who tries gets a dramatically higher cost per call. [How To Scale A Call Funnel]
- VSL length: short works for **no-brainer offers** (jump straight to the nuances/price/onboarding); longer is fine for offers people have never heard of (a real-estate offer needed a **40-minute VSL**). [Call Funnels That Make Rich People Buy]

### The application + scheduler tech (most-repeated tactical point)

> "We still today have not found any replacement for the Typeform [+] Calendly combo." [ONLY Guide]

- **Why**: Typeform has a native Calendly integration. The scheduler is embedded as a step inside the application via a logic tree, so a qualified applicant schedules without leaving the page. "To the lead it looks like this is just a step they have to accomplish in order to... submit their application."
- **The 50% drop-off rule**: if application and scheduler are on **separate pages/URLs** (e.g., GoHighLevel, HubSpot in-house), you lose ~**50%** of qualified applicants who don't schedule — effectively **doubling your cost per call**. [40-50 Qualified Calls / The ULTIMATE Funnel]
- **Integrated drop-off benchmarks**: Typeform+Calendly ≈ **16% average drop-off**; SalesKick with a *simple* application ≈ **16–20%**. [How To Scale A Call Funnel]
- Jeremy is not an affiliate; he "doesn't want to be right, [he] wants to make money" — Typeform+Calendly consistently produces the **cheapest cost per qualified call** test after test.
- **Alternatives he tracks** (as of filming): 
  - **iClosed** — sales-centric, allows double bookings, good visual analytics; used by higher-level inner-circle members; "good things." [How To Scale A Call Funnel]
  - **SalesKick** — promising features (calendar routing, double-book, AI lead-scoring) but **polarized reviews**. Its AI-analysis step on free-form answers adds a **4–6 sec up to 10–15 sec delay** before routing to the scheduler → drop-off worse than 50% because people think the page froze. Not currently recommended for free-form/AI-graded applications. [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

### Application questions
- Questions themselves signal buyer quality and feed qualification/pixel conditioning.
- **Tell on yourself**: asking "Did you watch the VSL in full?" or "Will you actually show up?" signals a weak back-end follow-up and a novice operator — well-framed leads make those questions unnecessary. [40-50 Qualified Calls]
- Disclose **price** in the application (and VSL, and emails). "Price is not something to object to. Price is something to make sense of." [Call Funnels for Rich People]

## 3. Messaging & who you attract (the "who" comes first)

> "Messaging is where everything starts in the attraction process." [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

Congruency is required across **ads → headline → VSL → application questions → confirmation page → emails → salespeople**. One wrong signal brings the wrong people (the *Inglourious Basterds* "three beers / wrong number three" analogy — one wrong signal and "all of a sudden you got a bunch of broke people coming through"). [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

### The 4 buyer types (risk spectrum)
1. **Group 1** — small, financially qualified, extreme risk-takers, first movers; need little/no groupthink or safety.
2. **Group 2** — need groupthink; want to feel early/cutting-edge. Message: "people have already been there, done that, but not that many — you're still early."
3. **Group 3** — need groupthink **and** safety in their dollars; they have less money.
4. **Group 4** — skeptics. "You never want to optimize around skeptics. You actively want to deter skeptics at all costs." [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

### In-market vs. needs-convinced (from Chet Holmes' *stadium selling*)
- **In-market**: actively seeking a solution now (small %). At small budgets you often get these first — lay-down deals.
- **Needs-convinced** (~**30%** of the market, per Jeremy): colder; can be convinced they have the problem/want the outcome. As you **scale**, leads get colder and costs rise, so you must **shift messaging toward education** ("why it makes sense to buy"). [How To Scale A Call Funnel]

### Messaging frameworks
- **Problems, Circumstances, Desired Outcomes** = the what/how/why of the prospect's world.
- **Master Messaging "bridge" SOP**: articulate where they *are* and where they *want to go* before they care about your bridge (offer); the bridge must feel sturdy. [How To Scale A Call Funnel]
- **DR ad structure**: Hook → Body → CTA. He built a **56-page** DR-ad creation framework for a client, plus a **6-page TL;DR** version. [ONLY Guide]
- **Avoid low-sophistication tactics** that attract skeptics/low-quality: audacious **income claims** and **guarantees**. "A guarantee does not create certainty... it signals to the people that are the most skeptical that they can have safety." Sophisticated/wealthier buyers read guarantees as an objection. [ONLY Guide]
- **Describe the ICP's conditions** rather than calling them out directly. Example: "If you're stuck around low couple thousand dollar a day in ad spend and every time you try to scale up... things fail... pay attention." [ONLY Guide]
- **Sell to rich people's vocabulary**: "capital" (not money), "liquidity"/"reserves" (not savings), "deploy cash/capital." [Call Funnels for Rich People]

## 4. Back-End Selling Systems (the 5) — fire after the call is booked

Jeremy says he introduced these "a few years ago." A call funnel on paid should **never** launch without them. All five **parrot the same messaging** because you don't know a given lead's consumption preference (some only watch videos, some only read emails; blue-collar niche ignores videos but loves emails). [Every $1M / How To Scale]

### 5.1 Confirmation page (breakout videos)
- Short intro video: "congrats, you booked; our job from here to the call is to help you do due diligence; expect emails + content; we want you in-or-out by call time." [ONLY Guide / Rich People]
- **Breakout videos**: one video per thing that (a) salespeople are tired of repeating, (b) you want clear before the call, or (c) are known aha/epiphany moments. Also cover objections, expectations, FAQs. **4 to 12+** videos, each ~**30 sec to 5 min**. One client has **19 breakout videos + 1 urgency video = 20 videos** on one page. [ONLY Guide / How To Scale]
- Gmail "unknown sender" fix (since early 2024): put a **screenshot of the calendar-invite email** at the top of the confirmation page (desktop screenshot on desktop page, mobile screenshot on mobile page) telling people to press "I know this sender" and accept the invite. [40-50 Qualified Calls]
- **Three separate short videos** often outperform one long video on the confirmation page: (1) nurture (sales-team feedback), (2) objections/FAQ, (3) case studies/expectations. [40-50 Qualified Calls]
- **Testimonials must be believable** — screenshotted from the source (e.g., Trustpilot), not prettified text with a stock photo. Should reflect the *average* result, placed **below** the breakout videos (breakout videos have more impact). ~**3/4 of the page** can be testimonials. [ONLY Guide / 40-50 Qualified Calls]
- **Testimonial spectrum** for rich buyers: show the good AND the bad experiences, and proactively address negative Reddit/Trustpilot threads — otherwise they research the downside themselves. [Call Funnels for Rich People]
- **Due-diligence / research video** ("add frame"): walk them through what they'd find on their own, including anything negative, with your narrative/justification attached. [How To Scale]
- Avoid lazy rhetoric ("make sure you show up," "don't waste our time") — "none of that [stuff] works nowadays." [How To Scale / The ULTIMATE Funnel]

### 5.2 Value-dense email sequences
- Framing/education emails (NOT reminders). **Up to 6 emails/day**, but **start low (2–4/day)** and add more while open rate stays healthy; stop when opens diminish. [How To Scale / Rich People / 40-50 Qualified Calls]
- Over a 72-hour booking window at 6/day = **18 emails** total. [ONLY Guide / 40-50 Qualified Calls]
- Target **open rates of 50–70%** (Jeremy cites 50–60% ideal, up to 60–70%+ when content is wanted). Judge primary-inbox delivery by the open rate, not just a 100% deliverability score. [ONLY Guide / How To Scale]
- Content = the real **questions, second-layer questions, objections, expectations** (the Hammer Them quadrants) that people actually care about.
- Book-funnel email tactic (low→high): summarize each chapter as an executive-summary email (12 chapters = 12-email sequence) + video/audio explanations to create aha moments fast. [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

### 5.3 Setter pre-call best practices
- Setters' job is to **do the education** part of the sale (not selfishly re-qualify financials). [How To Scale]
- Establish contact ASAP after assignment (ideally within the first few hours), mainly by **text** since people don't answer phones.
- **Engagement-bait text** + **selfie video** from a real **iPhone** (see §6). Example bait: "I've got a great set of pro forma links and video breakdowns per property... Would you prefer I text or email the list?" [How To Scale]
- **AI setters** emerging: client "Tom" (hyperbaric chambers, scaled to $10M/year with essentially no staff) uses an **ElevenLabs (11 Labs)** AI setter to run the education part on the phone. [How To Scale]

### 5.4 AI manipulation mastery
- People outsource critical thinking to AI; make sure Google AI Overview, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Manus say **positive** things about you.
- Five levers include **blogs, Trustpilot, Reddit** (+ two others taught in paid offers). [Every $1M / How To Scale]
- Real failure: Google AI Overview quoted a client's price as **$5,000** (pulled from a decade-old podcast for a different offer) when the real offer was **$17,000–$26,000** — "inverse price anchoring" that nuked close rate when prospects arrived. Audit what AI says about you. [How To Scale]

### 5.5 Trust assets / sales assets
- Long documents/pages packed with proof answering the sophisticated questions prospects ask. Don't need high production — a Loom going through the real data works. [ONLY Guide]
- Capital-raise example (via inner-circle member "Evan"): a single long doc with an executive-summary video, on-site full video, offer memorandum, deal financials, CoStar report, drone footage, "best place to live" articles, market-growth data, links. [ONLY Guide / How To Scale]
- "Nick" (Airbnb done-for-you): a sales-asset page listing **every deal** with address, two links each, full financial breakdown, and a video breakdown per property → got **show rate to 75%+**. [How To Scale]

## 5. The Hammer Them strategy (retargeting content engine)

The retargeting layer of the back-end system. Its purpose: improve **show rate, close rate, AOV**, and **shorten sales cycles**, by getting people all their answers so they don't research elsewhere. [40-50 Qualified Calls]

### The 4 content quadrants
1. **Questions** (about the vehicle/offer)
2. **Second-layer questions** (the questions that arise after the first answers — the "New Yorkers say their water is better → why? → soft water/minerals" example)
3. **Objections**
4. **Expectations** (cost, timeline, what's required, what happens after buying)

Map these on a Google Doc/whiteboard → should yield a **minimum of 30–50 pieces of content.** Questions should be **under 90 seconds**, vertical shorts/reels, posted to IG or run as dark posts. [40-50 Qualified Calls]

### Volume & format
- **30–50 short-form** pieces; **20–30 long-form** pieces. [Rich People / 40-50 Qualified Calls]
- Short-form + long-form go into **two separate engagement-based campaigns**, optimized for **through-play / video views**, **one video per ad set**. [How To Scale / Rich People]

### Technical setup
- Build a **dynamically updating custom audience** of "people who booked a call" (added on booking, auto-removed after the window via CRM/Zapier workflow).
- **Exclude 3-second video viewers** per ad set, so once someone sees a video for ≥3 sec they never see it again and rotate to the next. [40-50 Qualified Calls / Rich People]
- Platforms: Facebook + Instagram (TikTok for short-form; some use YouTube/LinkedIn). Also pile in people booking from YouTube/organic.

### Frequency targets (within the ~72-hour booking→call window)
- Overall Hammer-Them frequency: **15–20** (sometimes stated 15–20+). [Every $1M / 40-50 / How To Scale]
- For rich-people version: **15–20 unique impressions from short-form** AND **10–15 from long-form**. [Rich People]
- At frequency 15 ≈ 5 pieces/day; at 20 ≈ ~6–7 pieces/day over 72 hours. [40-50 Qualified Calls]

### Minimum audience size
- Need **≥100 people targetable** → realistically **150–200 booked/in-process** people to clear match-rate loss. If too small, extend the window or add people currently in the sales process / unclosed. [How To Scale / Rich People]

### Email arm of Hammer Them
- Same quadrants → **18 emails over 72 hours (6/day)**. [40-50 Qualified Calls]

### Execute it fully (the #1 failure)
People half-ass it (short-form only, no long-form, below frequency) and don't get the result. Done right across all steps, "it's damn near impossible not to have at least **60 to 70% show rates**." Also: the content must answer the *real* questions (take feedback from the sales team), not what you think people want. [ONLY Guide]
- **Reflection loop**: watch for a discrepancy between sales-team feedback and the content. Real example — a client had Hammer-Them **frequency of 24** in 72 hours but the content didn't match what the sales team said leads were stuck on → adjust/refilm content. The **leading KPI is salesperson feedback** (are leads showing up hotter/warmer/shorter cycle?). [40-50 Qualified Calls]

## 6. Show-rate: setter/closer pre-call communication

- **Communicate from a real iPhone**, not a CRM number or Android. Carriers now share which numbers are business/Twilio/CRM lines (like email spam filters) → those go to a **spam/filtered text folder**; third-party apps (bought mostly by rich people) block them. A real iPhone number hits the normal text inbox. [40-50 / Rich People]
- Rich people "think you're poor" / think it's a bot when texts come from green/Android/CRM numbers. [40-50 / Rich People]
- **Selfie video** from the rep (uncompressed iPhone-to-iPhone) is one of the easiest ways to stand out and "bait" a response. Example script: intro + "I made a video based on your application... can you shoot me a text so I know this is your number and I'll send it?" [40-50 / Rich People]
- Logic: once there's a human/face attached, people feel they'd be letting someone down by no-showing. **Higher pre-call engagement → higher show rate.**
- Blue-text-from-CRM software exists (~mid-couple-hundred $/line) but is still "janky" (~50/50 experiences); not yet endorsed. [40-50 / Rich People]
- Practical: put 5–7 closers on a **family/business plan** with financed iPhones; have them give equipment back on exit.

### The low show-rate diagnosis (recurring Dubai-tax story)
Jeremy's canonical example: he booked a call with a Dubai company promising to zero-out US taxes (booked **9 days out** — too far). They sent only generic reminder emails. He researched on his own, found the claim false, and no-showed.
> "If you have a low show rate it's because you are not giving people the necessary information pre-call." People in **justification mode** will research regardless — if the info doesn't come from you, they justify doing everything else (make-money activities) instead of your spend-money call. [40-50 / The ULTIMATE / How To Scale]

## 7. Booking window & cycle management

- **Max ~72 hours** from booking to call; **~24 hours is ideal** (enough time for consumption without decay). Show rate degrades the further out people book. [How To Scale]
- But if leads show up **under-educated** (closers complain "leads are too cold"), **add a little time** so back-end consumption can happen; or intentionally space booking to 2–3 days out. [How To Scale / ONLY Guide]
- **Cycle management** (rich people): they commit to a cycle and want to close it fast to free up mental "RAM." Dense back-end follow-up *shortens* the cycle for both sides — rich people move through cycles fast to take on more. [Call Funnels for Rich People]
- **Josh Troy's "10-day sales frame"**: on the call, get the prospect's commitment to be "in or out" within a set window (e.g., 10 days; some use 3), framed around respecting their time + shared values (be direct, honest, transparent). Shortens long sales cycles. [Call Funnels for Rich People]

## 8. Ads, pixel conditioning & the algorithm (for call funnels)

### Direct-response ads
- Simple talking-head DR ads: **60 sec–2 min** (shorter for rich audiences, a bit longer for less-financially-qualified). Structure: "we've got this offer / why people like you need it / CTA: talk to our team, schedule a call." [The ULTIMATE Funnel]
- Interest levels: leads enter **curious → general interest**; salespeople operate at **sold**. Marketing + sales must **incrementally bump interest up** level by level (usually 2–4 levels). [The ULTIMATE / 40-50 Qualified]
- Turn **off** Advantage+ creative toggles and **related media** (a client, "Devon," got a random client face injected into his feed). Keep only the "highlight a positive comment" toggle. [How To Scale]

### Standard events in a call funnel (for optimization)
- Page (headline+VSL+application) = **View Content**
- Application submitted = **standard event**
- **Schedule** = standard event ← Jeremy typically optimizes here
- Showed up = **custom conversion**
- Closed = **Purchase** (furthest down; ideal in theory but usually not enough volume)

### Pixel conditioning (the "moral of the story")
- **Only send qualified data back.** Fire **Schedule** and/or **Complete Registration** on the qualified confirmation page. At scale he uses both via **cyclic campaigns**. Don't send unqualified people (and often don't even send qualified *applications* unless they booked). [ONLY Guide / The ULTIMATE]
- If salespeople report that pixel-fired "qualified" people are actually unqualified: **withhold the automatic pixel event** and instead use the **Conversion API** tied to a CRM **"qualified" deal stage** that the rep moves people into — pushed back on a **short delay**. [ONLY Guide]
- Don't leave the results column empty too long or the algo rotates to a new audience. "It takes a couple people" of data to signal the algo — anyone claiming "it takes 50 or 100" is "operating like a barbarian." [ONLY Guide]

### Learning mode & "pocket audiences"
- Need **≥50 events per ad set per 7 days** to escape learning mode (sometimes a little less on small TAMs). Without it, audiences/stats swing weekly and you misread the data. [How To Scale]
- Loading 1 vs. 10 ad sets changes your ability to stay in the same **pocket audience**. Have budget ready to "gas it" on positive leading indicators.
- **Messaging + optimization event control who you reach first**; once data lands, the pixel's **recency bias** takes over. Example: "Abram" went viral on Facebook, got flooded with the wrong leads into the results column, and performance tanked despite months of good history. To change audiences, you can **starve the pixel** of data, launch a **new campaign/pixel**, or recondition. "Practice the art of detachment... be willing to walk away from a pixel." [How To Scale]

### Build ads from sales-call transcripts
- Feed your sales-call transcripts + SOPs (Master Messaging, Creative Diversification, DR Ad Creation) into an AI to generate ad ideas, ad copy, funnel copy, and VSL scripts. Use the **high-friction / good-question calls**, not just lay-downs, for better output. [How To Scale]

### AI sales manager
- AI reviews **every** call transcript vs. best practices/compliance, generates per-rep feedback (CRM/Slack/email), an **unbiased end-of-day report to the owner AND to the marketer**, and can flag likely future refunds/chargebacks from how a deal was closed. "AI does a far better job at sales management compared to human beings" at this scale. [How To Scale]

## 9. Benchmarks & financial model — generating 40–50 qualified calls/day → ~$1M/mo

From the dedicated 40-50 video, with a $250K/mo spend (range **$200K–$300K**) and **$200 cost per qualified call** (commonly **$150–$250** at scale). Cost per call deemed good can range **$50–$900** depending on downstream stats. [40-50 / The ULTIMATE]

**Shared inputs**: $250,000 spend ÷ $200 CPC = **1,250 calls/month**. ÷ ~20 selling days = **~62.5 booked calls/day** (shoot *above* the 40–50 goal). Show rate assumed **70%**. Cash collected (AOV) = **$7,500**. Agency/marketing cost ≈ **$15,000/mo**. Closer commission = **20%**.

### Two-call close model
| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Spend | $250,000 |
| Cost per call | $200 → 1,250 calls |
| Show rate (call 1) | 70% |
| Book 2nd call (of shows) | 65% |
| Show rate (call 2) | 60% |
| Close rate (call 2) | 38% |
| Cash collected / sale | $7,500 |
| Clients closed | **130** |
| Gross revenue | **$972,000** |
| Gross profit | **~$513,000** |

### One-call close model (Jeremy's bias)
| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Spend | $250,000 |
| Cost per call | $200 → 1,250 calls |
| Show rate | 70% |
| Close rate | 15% |
| Cash collected / sale | $7,500 |
| Clients closed | **131** |
| Gross revenue | **$984,000** |
| Gross profit | **~$522,000** |

> "Every additional step that you add to a marketing or sales process typically erodes the overall quantity of people that make it out the other side." — why he's biased to one-call closes. [40-50 Qualified Calls]

Both models land just shy of $1M; to cross it: spend a bit more, lower CPC, or lift show/close/AOV. **Build the financial model first**, overshoot the goal, and hold marketers & closers accountable to the KPIs. [40-50 / The ULTIMATE]

### Other call-funnel benchmarks (across videos)
- **Show rate**: lowest averages ~**50%**, highest ~**70–80%**; well-executed back-end → **60–70%+**. [The ULTIMATE / ONLY Guide]
- A higher AOV / higher cash-collected tolerates higher CPC, lower show rate, lower close rate.
- **Pixel-blocker rate**: ~**40–50%** of people block the pixel. [40-50 Qualified Calls]

### Scaling math (the two-lever flywheel)
> "Fill the closers' calendars. Hire more closers. Fill the closers' calendars. Hire more closers." [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

- Ask closers how many call slots/day are open vs. maxed. Max out slots, then **hire closers ahead of the cap** (recruit→train→shadow takes **~3–4 weeks**) so you don't stall scaling. The sales team naturally resists hiring until maxed — don't let them, or you lose 3–4 weeks of scale. [The ULTIMATE Funnel]
- Daily lead-flow funnels (call funnels) give **daily feedback** / tighter improvement cycles vs. lump-sum funnels.
- Highest clients run call funnels to **$5M/month**. [The ULTIMATE Funnel]

### Sales team requirements
- Proper **sales management** is mandatory: listen to recordings, daily training, per-lead depositions, articulate (not "the leads are bad") feedback relayed to marketing. One sales manager per account (max two). [40-50 Qualified Calls]
- Jeremy trusts essentially one outsourced sales agency: **Josh Troy / "Wires from Strangers"** (no referral $). Beware agencies whose "manager" runs 20 accounts. [40-50 Qualified Calls]
- Fire bottom closers (don't tolerate past ~a month); keep closers hungry.

## 10. The other funnel types (when to use / not use)

Ranked by Jeremy as how common they are on the road to $1M/mo. "Webinars and call funnels are the two main funnels." [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

### A) Webinars (live, and some scheduled-automated)
- Most popular because they create a warm/organic-like buyer and **condense the sales cycle** — good for weak sales teams.
- Sales cycle ~**7–14 days**. Financial loss for ~**2–3 days** during pre-webinar promo; spike **day-of/day-after**; decays over 7–14 days; **cleanup efforts** add revenue.
- **Promote live for ~3 days max** to maximize show rate.
- Expectation: **3–5x ROAS** when going well; when not, break-even to ~1.x (may lose a little after commissions).
- **Lump-sum funnel** (not daily lead flow): must spend enough to make it worth it, not so much it ruins you, and be repeatable ~**1–2×/week**. (Mountain-house client spent $10–15K/webinar for 3–5x, netted ~$20K, <$100K/mo in test phase — "not enough.")
- B2B can run mid-day; most run **~7pm ET**.

**Launch & scale method (dedicated video):**
- **Research first**: attend webinars by people crushing it (e.g., **Tom Cruz**, weekly Section-8 webinars for years), even outside your niche. Webinars are an **acquired skill** (group selling/public speaking).
- **Russell Brunson's "ask permission to go into the close"**: before pitching, ask the audience to "drop a 1 in the chat" to let you transition to the offer.
- **Audience types**: organic (most forgiving, **~30–40% show rate**), warm paid, and **cold paid (most judgmental, ~10–15% show rate**, but most scalable).
- **House money strategy**: first run to organic/warm audiences to bank profit, then spend that "house money" to dial in cold paid (a burn period) before scaling.
- **>$5K offers → book a call** off the webinar (not direct-to-checkout), because personal/competitive questions only get answered 1:1.
- **Worked example math**: $20,000 budget, $20 CPL = **1,000 registrants**; 10% show = **100 live**; ~70% still there at the **~60-min pitch** (90-min webinar) = **70**; 20% book = **14 calls**; 50% show = **7**; close 20–30% = **2–3 sales** — shows why cold webinars are higher risk and need optimization.
- **Real case**: $70K real-estate info offer; webinar #1 = profitable (~$40K gross, ~$10K pocketed after 20% commission); webinar #2 failed (rep rebuilt the deck into a **3-hour** webinar, wrong energy, **1 sale**); after **~5 webinars at $20K each** reaching ~break-even/just above, they scaled the **6th to $80K**. Lesson: make **minor iterations**, keep webinars to **~90 min**, show up in the right energy state; **scale only after leading indicators confirm it's dialed in.**
- **Can automate** only once a live webinar is proven; automated = scheduled video at set times (not just-in-time), sometimes daily/twice-daily.
- **Webinars are the highest-risk funnel** (most steps that can break it + infrequent iteration) and have the **highest intimidation factor**. KPIs chain: CPM, link CTR, opt-in rate, show rate, retention rate, booking rate, call show rate, close rate, AOV.

### B) Call funnels
Covered in detail above (§1–§9). Best on the **organic** side ("always run a call funnel" organically); on **paid**, only with a dialed-in team and the **back-end selling systems** (non-negotiable). Daily lead flow → easy scaling flywheel.

### C) Low-ticket → high-ticket
- Adds length to the sales cycle. Best low-ticket offer is **broken out of the high-ticket offer** (e.g., Jeremy's **$750 "Jeremy's Master Classes"** cherry-picked from the high-ticket weekly inner-circle group-call vault). [Every $1M/Month Funnel]
- Must have **speed to consumption** + engineer fast **aha moments** (nobody buys low then immediately buys high — they extract value first).
- Compress the cycle via **emails + setters + retargeting** (chapter-summary email sequences; exclusive low-ticket-buyer content).
- **Tolerance = profitability**: book funnel losing 20–40¢/dollar with a 3-month cycle = intolerable; a slightly-profitable ($750 at ~2:1) front end makes a 1–3-month cycle tolerable. Often a **supplementary** funnel unless front-end profitable or willing to break even.
- KPI chain: CPM, link CTR, sales-conversion rate of the page, post-purchase AOV steps, then (if ascending to calls) application/show/close/AOV. Sales pages are harder to get working than "free" funnels (call/webinar).

### D) DM ads / organic DMs
- Least reliable for $1M/mo. Usually **caps around $300K** (most hit $100–300K then plateau).
- Setter-team cap ~**3–5** before the system breaks (sales management + weaker setter talent).
- Only two people Jeremy's seen exceed it: **Brian Mark** (organic DM, posting 3–10×/day with Manychat → human setters) and inner-circle member **"Kay"** (~**$1.5M/mo**: paid content distribution → DMs, **3-month cycle, $3,000 AOV**, direct-to-checkout via setters, fronting ~$500K/mo ad spend ≈ **3:1**).
- Major weakness: **blind-as-a-bat tracking** → people won't scale spend blindly. IG is a "scary place to scale" (bans/restrictions). Good for the **chunk method**. [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

### E) Challenge funnels (3-day virtual events)
- **Largest lump-sum funnel**; run **quarterly at best**, usually **annually/twice a year** (exhaustive).
- **Burn period 60–90 days**; e.g., spend ~**$500K** in the burn to make **$2–5M** lump sum. You can liquidate 50–80% of acquisition cost (or break even) going in; bigger brand = closer to profitable in the burn.
- Best framed as a **massive, efficient cleanup tool** on top of your daily lead-flow funnels. Inner-circle member **"Smitty"** got a $1M/mo trophy from a challenge funnel, profiting ~**$800K**. [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

### F) Organic (as its own funnel type)
- Technically a traffic source, but classified separately. **Very long cycle (often 3–6 months)** → **stronger leads**; compounding effect (bullwhip ~3–6 months later).
- Acts immediately as a **sales nurture asset** and follow-up tool for paid leads. "Start organic sooner than later." Like a "steroid/booster" for everything else.

### The chunk method
When one funnel hits its scale ceiling before your revenue goal, **stack** another funnel on top (e.g., Jeremy's own $1M/mo recurring = mostly **Jeremy's Inner Circle** + minority **Jeremy AI**). Ceilings aren't a fixed number; usually one funnel amplifies to the goal, but the chunk method bridges gaps. [Every $1M/Month Funnel]

## 11. Quick selection cheat-sheet

| Funnel | Best for | Cadence | Risk | Key stat/benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Call funnel** | High-ticket ($5K+), dialed-in or organic sales team | Daily lead flow | Lower | 70% show, 15% (1-call) / 38% (2-call, 2nd) close, $200 CPC, $7.5K AOV |
| **Webinar** | Weaker sales teams, warm-able buyer | 1–2×/week (live), lump sum | **Highest** | 3–5x ROAS; cold show 10–15%, organic 30–40% |
| **Low→High** | Front-end-profitable ascension | Continuous, long cycle | Medium | tolerance = profitability; break low-ticket out of high-ticket |
| **DM ads/organic DM** | Chunk-method add-on | Continuous | High (blind tracking) | caps ~$300K; setter cap 3–5 |
| **Challenge (3-day)** | Cleanup lump sum | Quarterly–annual | High (big burn) | burn 60–90 days, ~$500K → $2–5M |
| **Organic** | Nurture/compounding under everything | Always-on | Low (slow) | 3–6 month cycle, strongest leads |
