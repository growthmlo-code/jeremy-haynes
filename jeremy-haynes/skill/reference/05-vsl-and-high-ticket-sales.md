# PART A — THE $1M/MONTH VSL

## A1. What a VSL is and does

A VSL is "a curated presentation where you've organized information in a particular flow with call to actions and strategic information to help validate your claims and provide certainty" [2026 VSL]. The core design principle: **a VSL is a normal sales conversation captured on video, just without the counterparty talking back.** "The perfect vssl ideally is just a flow like a normal sales conversation but done without the counterparty communicating with you" [$1M VSL].

The reason the flow works is that each step answers the question a real prospect would ask next. Skip a step and you create a gap: "if you have a question that's left unanswered... it'll be as if they didn't hear a single word you said past the point when their question came up" [$1M VSL].

## A2. The canonical VSL script flow (in order)

Jeremy walks through his VSL SOP. The beats, in order:

**Step 1 — The Hook / Opener = what you can do for them.**
Open by stating exactly what you'll help them with. Example he gives: "hey guys my name is Jeremy Haynes I'm going to help you master internet marketing if you're looking to become more certain as a digital marketer I'm your guy" [$1M VSL]. A steep drop-off at the very start means "your opener sucks and you're not immediately telling people why they should stick around" [$1M VSL]. Fix the open, don't blame the audience.

**Step 2 — Why you (why you're the one to deliver it).**
Immediately follow the hook with why you/your company can get that outcome: background, expertise, track record, proof. Must be a *genuine* reason, like a real conversation — not filler "as featured in Forbes/Fox" fluff that "nobody would actually care about in real life" [$1M VSL]. This is also where you strategically plant early objection-handlers (trust, money justification) using real numbers, e.g. client count, dispute/refund rate [$1M VSL]. Steps 1 and 2 can be short (sometimes under a minute).

**Step 3 — Sell the bigger picture / buying motives (the "majority hook").**
Talk through the actual reasons people buy, cascading from the **majority hook** (the #1 bulk driver of purchases) down through secondary reasons to minority hooks. His window-tint example: most buy to reduce heat/glare (majority), a few to look cool, a few to commit crimes (minority — which you'd never market) [$1M VSL]. This is usually a large chunk of the VSL — multiple slides — where you elaborate on each dominant buying motive. Mirrors the point in a real call where you'd ask "what's your specific reason" and then say "we help that exact type of person."

**Step 4 — Present the offer + STATE THE PRICE.**
Present the single main thing you want to sell (sell "whatever is going to make you the most money that the most people... are going to buy"); you can briefly mention you also have cheaper/pricier fringe options [$1M VSL]. **Always say the price.** His inarguable stance: saying price qualifies the buyer, and qualified buyers fire the pixel so the ad algorithm finds more of them — "I Through Time Out compete you every time in getting highly qualified people" [$1M VSL]. Explain what's included. **Never use value stacks** (see A5).

**Step 5 — Objection handling.**
Transition in ("I think this is a perfect offer, let me go through why") and handle the *real* objections pulled from real sales calls — not frivolous made-up ones. "This is the place to have the sales conversation" [$1M VSL]. For affluent buyers, handle the hard-hitting real objections (unhandled = lower show rate / more leavers). For general public, handle more objections and spend longer. This is often another large chunk of the VSL.

**Step 6 — Qualification (who it's for / who it's NOT for).**
Explicitly state who should act and who should leave, so the right person self-identifies and the wrong person disqualifies. His channel itself is the example: he openly tells "small fries" to go watch other content — qualifying by repelling [$1M VSL].

**Step 7 — Clear call to action.**
Make the single CTA explicit (e.g., "fill out the application below, if you're qualified you'll be able to schedule a call"). Nothing else to over-explain [$1M VSL].

**Optional closer — Testimonials + final CTA.**
You may pepper in highly believable testimonials then repeat the CTA, *as long as* testimonials are believable and you don't exceed the demographic's ideal length [$1M VSL].

## A3. The 2026 "Summary Section" pro-tip (updated opener)

In the **first 60–90 seconds, say everything the entire VSL covers** — "like the tweet version of what you otherwise might say in an expanded newsletter" — then expand on each point for the rest of the video [2026 VSL]. On his own Inner Circle page the VSL "says the price, like literally just straight away, and then I jump into everything people want to know in that first 60 to 90 seconds" [2026 VSL]. This serves the efficiency/energy-conservation instinct and lets people decide the moment they feel certain. For a long VSL to rich people, this summary tip is mandatory.

## A4. Length, by audience

| Audience | Ideal length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Affluent / rich** | 2–15 min (he's seen 2–5 min "absolutely rip") | Direct, concise, concentrated info; shorter + more direct = better fit [$1M VSL; 2026 VSL; Sell To Rich] |
| **General public** | 5–10 min to test; up to **60 min max** | More skepticism = more trust assets = more length; some run a near-full webinar in the VSL slot [$1M VSL] |
| **2026 observed average** | ~10–20 min across the board | "It's all over the place currently" — test widely [2026 VSL] |

Capital-raising example: "these people typically have a 2-minute vsl... their ad videos are usually 45 seconds to 60 seconds" [$1M VSL]. General-public outlier: a client runs "a literal podcast, like unedited" an hour long and the public responds well [2026 VSL]. Beyond 60 min, retention/view-rate metrics fall off hard.

## A5. Affluent vs. General Public — VSL style rules

**Affluent demographic:**
- Direct, concise, authoritative, high (authentic) certainty; "no fluff, no added yapping."
- Claims must be **conservative / undershot**, never overzealous but still believable.
- Presentation design should be **simple and NOT overdone** — white background/black text or vice versa, few images, often no logo; a mini-webinar screen-recording with voiceover. Overdone design "flops" with the affluent (like the flashy person trying to look richer than they are) [$1M VSL].
- Real scarcity usually not needed — they're biased toward action and risk.

**General public:**
- More skepticism → front-load **trust assets**; be more **empathetic**, less blunt (too direct reads as "an asshole").
- Presentation **must be well-designed / stylized** — doing the bare-bones affluent style here reads as a scam.
- Claims can be **overzealous** — "the more overzealous the claim is typically the better it performs" — taken "to the greatest compliant extent" [$1M VSL].
- Believable discounts work (e.g. $5,000 → $3,500). Real scarcity works well. They are **savers, not earners**; you're "selling against the thing that they've been saving for" or against credit-card debt — acknowledge this empathetically inside the VSL [$1M VSL].

## A6. The "character" rule (filming yourself)

About half of Jeremy's clients who hit $1M months use faceless screen-recorded presentations; the other half show themselves [$1M VSL]. If you appear on camera you must **match the character the buyer expects** — dress, environment, tone like "a chameleon." Capital-raisers wear quality custom suits, neutral offices. The Gucci-jumpsuit cautionary tale: a $40K-offer client attracted unserious leads in a flashy Gucci jumpsuit; switching to a neutral black tee + track pants + neutral Nikes — *same targeting, same messaging* — overnight brought "a far greater quantity of serious qualified people" [$1M VSL]. Look the part for both affluent and general public.

## A7. Reading VSL analytics

- **Retention/engagement graph shape:** a gradual descending decline is natural and healthy (people conclude "this is for me," then act or leave). A **steep drop at the very beginning = redo the opener** or you've tapped a minority hook and need to broaden to a majority hook [$1M VSL]. Don't expect anyone to watch 100% — "that's the dumbest thing ever."
- **Play rate** = % who hit the page and press play. 30%+ is good; he's hit $1M months on a **9% play rate** (strong qualifier headlines repel the wrong people). Blue-collar niches run single-digit/teens play rates — then convert the VSL content into on-page sections instead [$1M VSL].
- **Engagement rate** = average % of the video consumed. Higher play rate = good. But a *very* high engagement rate can be bad: it may mean people "are having a hard time understanding what you can actually do for them" and must watch more [2026 VSL]. Tighter articulation can lower engagement while raising conversions.

## A8. 2026 technical pro-tips

- **Video chaptering:** add chapters so viewers scrub to what they care about — helps retention/consumption because "nobody actually wants to watch it in its entirety" [2026 VSL].
- **Withhold the apply button?** Only if (a) your bank account can absorb inflated cost-per-call while the pixel re-learns, and/or (b) your sales team requires people to have watched content first. Withholding cuts schedulers, inflates cost-per-qualified-call, and sends less data back to the pixel (breaks pixel-conditioning / staying out of learning phase). If your sales team is strong ("a set of dogs"), don't withhold [2026 VSL].
- **Withhold the play bar?** If you withhold the button, also withhold the play bar (otherwise someone scrubs to the end and has nothing to act on). Hiding the bar while showing the button *lowers* intent (forces people to sit through). [2026 VSL]
- **Hosting:** use **Wistia** (his top pick for analytics/visualization) or **Vidalytics**. Avoid YouTube/Vimeo. [$1M VSL; 2026 VSL]
- **Thumbnails:** video thumbnails "absolutely rip" over static images (higher play rates); if using an image (for page speed), still show a real key moment the user cares about. Never the cheesy "stop, don't press skip / press play" thumbnail — "those always perform like shit" [$1M VSL; 2026 VSL].
- **Page layout:** keep the page minimal — **headline + VSL + application/button only**. Extra sections let people self-conclude by scrolling instead of pressing play, lowering play rate. "If you want more people to watch the video, put less things on the page" [2026 VSL].
- **Editing:** jump cuts + captions is the floor; adding visual edits, music, occasional memes (like his YouTube style) aids retention because "people's brains are fried" [2026 VSL].
- **Styles that work in 2026 (roughly even mix):** (1) talking head, (2) slides/screen-recording with voiceover, (3) Google-doc/slide on left + talking head on right reading along. No single style dominates [2026 VSL].

# PART B — HIGH-TICKET SALES & CLOSING

Built from the FULL 4-HOUR COURSE [If I Wanted To Master Sales] and [How To ACTUALLY Sell To Rich People]. Jeremy frames the course against the **"fat cat closer"**: "an entitled rep that refuses to do anything other than take layup deals scheduled straight onto their calendar" [4-Hour Sales Course]. The whole system is about turning reps into real salespeople — and, where marketing is done right, "I turn sales people into cashiers" [$1M VSL].

## B1. The updated lead model & first contact (speed-to-lead)

Consumer reality: people keep "Do Not Disturb" / "silence unknown callers" on and never call back missed numbers. So:

1. **Text first, with your name.** Texting "hey it's Jeremy" before you call tricks iPhone to show "Maybe: Jeremy," raising answer rates [4-Hour Sales Course].
2. **Call at least twice.** On DND, the 1st call goes to voicemail, the 2nd rings through.
3. **Speed matters:** contacting within ~5 minutes raises close/engagement probability by "a full 50% probability" [4-Hour Sales Course].
4. **Open-ended multi-option text to reduce the "no."** His exact template: *"hey Leo it's Jeremy saw you just filled out a form of ours online would love to chat with you if you are open in the next 5 minutes if not I have some time later today let me know if that works or shoot over whatever question you have"* — three options turns a 50/50 yes/no into a ~75% chance of positive engagement [4-Hour Sales Course].

## B2. Team structure: Setter / Closer / Cleaner

- **Closer-only works at first**, but activity compounds. Week 1 = just call the day's new leads; week 2 adds last week's scheduled follow-ups + new leads; by ~week 3–4 it's mathematically impossible for closers to do all three core jobs: **(1) scheduled/follow-up calls, (2) new daily leads, (3) following up with past leads** [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **Setter/closer model:** needed when lead volume/activity breaks those three jobs. Critically, **Setters must be trained to answer real questions or extract data**, not just book — the McLaren/speed-tail analogy: a setter who can't help a $3.2M buyer loses the customer; a setter who takes notes and promises the closer will follow up pre-appointment *raises* show rate [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **The Cleaner (his favorite role):** a (usually closing) role that re-establishes communication with older leads no longer being followed up. Reclaims money left on the table; great training ground — promote them to full Setter or Closer once sharp [4-Hour Sales Course].

## B3. In-house vs. outsourced sales teams

Jeremy strongly prefers **in-house**. Outsourced closing teams have misaligned incentives, go part-time, are full of fat-cat closers, won't dial inbound form leads, won't follow up old leads, and only take scheduled calls. His war story: an outsourced team closed well for 2 weeks, then fired the client and refunded the $20K to move closers to a 6-deals/day account — "very fair" incentive-wise, but it leaves the client stranded [4-Hour Sales Course]. In-house teams carry the company's incentives and do everything (cold outbound, inbound dials, scheduled calls, follow-up).

## B4. The core sales-call structure

**Open with the PRICE, then justify value after.** This is the single most-repeated closing mechanic across the course.

His word track: *"I just want to be transparent with you at the beginning of this call — our offer is about $40,000... if I show you why it makes sense to make that type of investment, do you think you could spend the next 15 minutes with me... or is it too much of a sticker shock?"* [4-Hour Sales Course].

Why price-first:
- Every feature/pain-point you cover *after* the price raises perceived value against a known number ("the Lamborghini Aventador" analogy — once you know it's $300K, every cool fact justifies it).
- Price-last creates a cliff — "there's nothing that can follow the price to justify why it costs that much" [4-Hour Sales Course].
- It "gets the shitty people off the phone" immediately — "shuffle the jokers out of the deck" [4-Hour Sales Course; guest Jordan Stupar].

**Conviction / interest-level law:** a sale only happens when both parties reach the same level of agreement. The prospect enters with low interest; **you must sustain high conviction and never capitulate down to their level** — if you waver, "you will be pulled down to their level and no deal occurs." You raise their interest one feature/objection at a time until interests meet [4-Hour Sales Course].

## B5. Discovery & the three P's (open strong)

Guest framework Jeremy endorses in-course: **"the best close is a strong open."** A bad salesman avoids objections; a good one overcomes them; **a great one knows the objections before the lead raises them and beats them to it** [4-Hour Sales Course · guest Troy Joshua].

- **Three P's of Pain:** identify the **Pain**, the **Problem** it causes, and the **Pressure** creating urgency to solve it. Without all three early, you likely won't get the deal [guest Troy Joshua].
- **Current state → Desired state:** the pitch is just the bridge between the two [guest Troy Joshua].
- **Talk less:** top closers talk <40% of the call; average ~58%; worst reps "word vomit." Keep a running list of questions to ping-pong back [guest Jordan Stupar].
- **Sell the main pillars, not the details** — people buy the sizzle; over-explaining details talks you out of deals [guest Troy Joshua].

## B6. Asking hard questions the right way (value-added vs. selfish)

To get bank balance / credit score, never ask selfishly. **Tie a reason to the question.** Lee's roofing example reframe: *"a lot of what we recommend will cost money... before we even consider working with somebody we'd like them to be transparent... how much capital you have in your operating accounts and what your credit score is so we can get you approved for funding... is that fair?"* End with **"is that fair?"** to keep it open-ended [4-Hour Sales Course]. Model: sell *why* you need the info (like pipe.com requiring bank connection + reconciled books + 650 credit before funding), then ask.

## B7. Show-rate system (sell the call + commitment)

Above-average orgs don't just rely on automation + a "you still showing up?" text — they **sell the call** and get a **verbal commitment** [4-Hour Sales Course]:
- **Sell the call:** tell them exactly what will happen and why it's worth their time; attach a **fast-action incentive they actually want** (a real discount, an added offer element, or a bonus 6th thing — note you can present something already included as if it's a show-up bonus).
- **Verbal commitment:** "can I get your word?" — "surprisingly about half the people who say they're going to show up tomorrow don't."
- **Selfie-video reminders** beat generic manual follow-ups for the let-down bias.
- Combined, these tactics lift show rates ~20–30% on average — "there is no magic pill... it's a combination" [4-Hour Sales Course].

## B8. Selfie videos (connection-rate weapon)

Pull out an iPhone, shoot a 10–30 sec selfie video on first contact / follow-up / reminder. "It's much harder to ignore a real person than it is to ignore an autonomous text" [4-Hour Sales Course]. Guest Jordan Stupar's stat frame: within 5 min = +50% conversion odds; differentiates vs. lazy reps; "+400% customer engagement." **Use iPhone, not Android** (Android compresses video to "absolute dog shit" quality) [Sell To Rich; 4-Hour Sales Course].

## B9. Sales-aid videos & assignment selling

Turn your most-common questions into short 1–3 min videos. Answer the question in text first, then offer the video for a lengthier answer — never force the video ("it takes me 10 seconds to read your text, one full minute to watch a video") [4-Hour Sales Course]. Feed recurring questions back to marketing to produce these. **Assignment selling** (guest): if a lead hasn't consumed content, it's the rep's job to walk them up the content gradient mid-process; aim for **commitment over compliance** [guest Troy Joshua].

## B10. Value-driven follow-up (and "perfect timing")

Never do selfish follow-up ("did you see my last message / got time to talk?"). **Every follow-up must deliver real value** — platform newsroom/algorithm-update articles, a new case-study/testimonial link, a relevant podcast with a timestamp ("skip to the 57-minute mark, that's where the money is"), a selfie video, press, or a real-scarcity note [4-Hour Sales Course]. Build "an arsenal" of such assets. Cadence: **weekly**, prioritizing leads you most want.

For rich prospects specifically: expect to be ignored — it's **not** disinterest. They get pulled away by higher priorities. Keep doing value-driven follow-up for **"perfect timing"** — "the sky clears," their schedule opens, they remember why they were interested, and they re-engage (Ryan Serhant closes some deals after 2–3 years of follow-up) [Sell To Rich].

## B11. AOV / incentives / fast-action

Incentives control behavior. Structure payment so the behavior you want is cheapest and best:
- **Pay-in-full** gets the most product + lowest price + bonuses; **split-pay** costs more and strips bonuses (his 5K product: $5,000 in full w/ 2 bonus courses vs. 2×$2,600 = $5,200, no bonuses) [4-Hour Sales Course].
- Killed the **quad-pay** (cards failed on payments 2–4); replaced with **funding/financing partners** (coachfinance, Finicity-type, 0% interest cards up to ~$50K avg, partners to $100–150K) — still collect full price, give the pay-in-full bonuses since you're paid in full.
- **Fast-action urgency must be REAL** — tax-deadline write-offs, Q4 activity bumps, limited allocation (capital raises close), "we incentivize action takers" with real price step-ups (e.g., +10% after 30 days, +10% more after 60). "If somebody does not care about an incentive they will not follow the incentive" [4-Hour Sales Course].

## B12. Objection handling

**Jeremy's principle:** pull the *real* objections from *real* calls; handle them proactively in the VSL and on the call. Guest Jordan Stupar's reusable formula: **ART + ?** — **A**cknowledge, **R**eply, **T**ransition away with a question [4-Hour Sales Course · guest].

Jeremy's own word-track library from the course:

- **"Can I speak to an existing client?"** — "we'd love to; want me to ring them now or set a time?" For busy/rich clients, instead send a long list of well-documented testimonials, give 24 hours, and book a follow-up. Optionally pay existing clients a commission to take reference calls.
- **"Any discounts?"** — reframe that it's *already* discounted (appreciating-asset / ground-floor / aged-store logic). If they push, **strip components**: "if you want it for $30k instead of $50k, we take away these two things." Never just cut price.
- **"I'm looking at others / I'll get back to you."** — "that's fair"; send a recap email, try to book a follow-up call. "You want the deal but you don't need the deal"; high-ticket often closes on the follow-up, not call one.
- **"I can't afford this."** — find out if it's cash or credit; walk through funding/financing options, then internal payment plans.
- **"How can you guarantee I'll make money?"** — info product: "it's up to you / you taking action" (disqualify non-action-takers). Automation: real money-back guarantee (asset appreciates as aged store → easy buy-back). Investor: can't guarantee (regulation/markets) — reframe via risk-tolerance comparables and show how you stack the odds.
- **"How is my money secure / are you legit?"** — **credibility assets** (press, podcasts, screenshot testimonials shown where captured), third-party audits = **"authority hijacking."**
- **"Can I speak to the owner / sit down face-to-face?"** — justified at high ticket; otherwise offer a weekly group Zoom with the CEO; or call out the real reason ("do you not trust us? what's holding you back?").
- **"I saw a negative review."** — bury it under believable positive testimonials; name the specific situation honestly; if you genuinely have many bad reviews, fix delivery + do SEO.
- **"I didn't expect it to cost that much."** — "I bet you won't expect it to make as much as it does for you either" → "costs what it costs because we make people a lot more than that, hear me out for 10 minutes."
- **"I'd rather start next quarter."** — reiterate real incentives/reasons; if they don't bite, ask directly "what's missing that would make you pull the trigger today?"
- **"Why are you better than everyone else?"** — you must actually be better; use the hypercar-comparable reframe (all great, subtly different) and bias to fit/preference.
- **"My business partner and I need to review."** — book a joint follow-up call with the partner now; pre-answer the partner's likely objections.
- **"What if you go out of business?"** — bonds/insurance (sophisticated) or a risk-tolerance word-track; disqualify the overly risk-averse.
- **"My lawyer needs to review."** — not really an objection; book the follow-up for when they'll actually move; reiterate time-boxed incentives.

Guest Jordan Stupar's deeper objection play: **the subconscious reframe** — people object because they compare you to their *bad* past decisions, not their good ones; redirect them ("when's the last time you took a chance and got a result you were happy with?" / "when's the last time you regretted paying a little more for a better service?") to pull the decision into the "neocortex" [4-Hour Sales Course · guest].

## B13. Managing, training & keeping reps great

- **Ongoing training is mandatory** — like pro athletes who never stop training. New hires should shadow senior reps (restaurant two-waiter model), watch past calls, get a base salary for the first 30–60 days.
- **Daily rhythm (modeled on Grant Cardone's org):** watch training videos before taking calls → a **morning meeting with real transparent stats** (not fluff) → **30–45 min of role-play** → then calls [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **Review game footage:** like the NBA bench coach cutting clips live — no "I don't have time" excuses. Individual review = individual improvement; collective review = collective advancement. "It's a Fool's game to learn the hard way... a wise man's game to learn hard lessons through others" [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **Personal life affects professional life:** track reps' moods; help with money/logistics stressors (he bought a struggling rep a Mercedes; that month +~300% in sales). David Meltzer's Friday non-business personal-development sessions as a model.
- **When to fire (Grant Cardone method):** track everyone statistically; each Friday review declining reps; give ~4 weeks of leeway with support; cut the bottom ~10% monthly *only if* their stats trend down. Wasted leads = lost ad spend, not just a rep's loss [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **Keep morale HIGH** — "sales do not happen when sales people have low morale." Morale shifts fast (the Miami Heat 4th-quarter run analogy); change the environment (hype music, acknowledging momentum/wins) to lift it [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **Tools:** CRMs for salespeople = **close.com** (don't use its built-in phone numbers — spam flags) or **Pipedrive**; marketing CRMs = ActiveCampaign / HubSpot / Keap (InfusionSoft). Slybroadcast for mass voicemail drops; local-area-code dialers raise connection rates to ~60% (vs. 20–30%) [4-Hour Sales Course].
- **Recruiting:** post descriptive job ads in high-ticket-closer Facebook groups (e.g., "High Ticket Ninjas"), vet profiles, ask for "Flex data/stats," then run 2 Zoom interviews with live role-play and scenario questions (e.g., "here's a lead list of 100 — what would you do?") to spot fat-cat closers vs. real salespeople [4-Hour Sales Course].

## B14. Selling to rich people specifically [How To ACTUALLY Sell To Rich People]

Everything optimizes around **two outcomes only: making them more money (turning money into more money) or saving them time.** Every communication centers on one or both.

1. **Be direct.** They skip sentence-casing, punctuation, perfect grammar — not because they're dumb, but "100% centered around... trying to save time." Communicate short, punchy, Twitter-length; elaborate only if asked. Don't read their terseness as rudeness.
2. **Save time = sell via writing first.** Rich inbound leads (from forms/ads) are **curious, not cold**, and prefer to vet via **text/email/DM**, not calls. Teams that assume "no call = not interested" lose huge money. Under-50s prefer text; over-50s may prefer calls (still fine to call, but text first). Use **iPhone** (blue bubbles; CRM/Android green bubbles get judged instantly; iPhone enables quality selfie videos and contact-card/"Maybe: Jeremy" tricks).
3. **Build trust rapidly** — they decide in minutes, so be **excessively transparent**. Deploy **sales assets** fast: decks, PPMs, team bios, even weekly criminal background checks, and the **"Rolodex"** (past investors who consented to share returns/amounts) — far more believable than standard testimonials (which must have real names + linkable social proof or they're assumed fake).
4. **Be selfless, not selfish.** Don't give up after one unanswered call; endure multi-week cycles; do **value-driven follow-up**.
5. **Expect to be ignored → value-driven follow-up → "perfect timing."** (see B10).
6. Conservative, believable claims (vs. overzealous for the public). Scarcity usually unnecessary — they're action/risk-biased.
