## 1. The core thesis: recurring revenue over transactional dollars

Jeremy's whole model is built on preferring recurring revenue to one-off transactional income. He credits his mentor Judge Graham for teaching him this early.

- He contrasts himself with clients whose "entire business, their million-plus a month in revenue... comes from transactional dollars" — and says that model "has always really scared me." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]
- He learned the importance of recurring revenue "at the very beginning of my career when I first met Judge." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]
- Reality check he repeats as a disclaimer: per research he cites, the odds of ever hitting $10M/year are 0.1%, and hitting $1M/month ($12M/year) is lower still. He makes no income claims.

He runs essentially two separate businesses under this philosophy:
1. An **education company** (recurring MRR products) — covered in video 027.
2. A **marketing agency** on a **revenue-share model** — covered in videos 039 and 054.

## 2. How he hit $1M/month in RECURRING revenue (the education company)

### 2.1 The two products that stack to $1M/month MRR
At the time of filming, he was at roughly $1M/month MRR = $12M ARR, from two products that he believes can EACH independently reach $1M/month:

**Product A — Jeremy's Inner Circle** (~$880,000/month at filming)
- Price: **$10,000/month** (recurring).
- Price history: it climbed over time — "1,500 a month to 2K a month to 3K a month to 4K a month to 5K a month to 6K a month to 10K a month where they are today." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]
- Originated in **2019** (~7 years old at filming) — explicitly NOT an overnight success.
- What's included: twice-a-month one-on-one calls (30 min each); weekly group call (Saturdays 11am ET); 4 in-person masterminds per year (top of each quarter — Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct — in Miami); very active group chat; a separate exclusive "million a month" group chat for higher earners; 35+ SOPs; a large course vault; DM access; and it also includes Jeremy AI.

- Income restrictions to join (it is "for rich people trying to get way richer than they are right now").
- Variant: **Jeremy's Half Circle** — strips the in-person mastermind (watch remote/live or recordings) and has no one-on-one calls, but keeps DM access.
- Discounts: fast-action bonuses; PIF (paid-in-full) discounts for paying the full annual upfront.

**Product B — Jeremy AI** (~$120,000/month at filming)
- Price: **$1,000/month** (recurring).
- An "agentic clone" of Jeremy — not just a chatbot; it can take actions. Connects to 10,000+ tools, ad accounts, CRMs, team chats, sales-call transcripts.

- Growth driver: mainly Instagram, secondary YouTube.
- His math on why it can reach $1M/month alone: "$1,000 per person per month, a thousand people sounds... easy and fair enough." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]

880 + 120 = ~$1M/month recurring.

### 2.2 The retention/leverage flywheel (why the model compounds)
A central, non-obvious lesson: **the delivery work on Inner Circle generates the training data for Jeremy AI.** Every one-on-one call, weekly group call, DM, voice note, mastermind talk, and private conversation becomes training material.

- "I get paid to create the training data for Jeremy AI, which in turn is a clone of me, which gets literally better and better as time goes on, and I get paid on that, too." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]
- He stresses this was NOT a master plan — he became aware of the leverage as AI progressed.

### 2.3 Pricing leverage lesson: raise prices as you approach capacity
A lesson he says he never hears others discuss:
- "When you get close to being maxed out, you gain more leverage to get paid more money to continue doing what you're doing." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]
- Getting paid much more to keep doing something near capacity tends to RAISE your capacity ceiling — "Your capacity ceiling raises coincidentally when you get paid a lot more money." He also reinvests the higher price into making the offer (and himself) better.

### 2.4 Offer–narrative alignment (why these offers sell now)
Offers must align to what the market currently wants ("current narratives"):
- Inner Circle sells because "business owners are tired of getting [screwed] over by agencies. Nobody wants the done-for-you work anymore." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue] Owners got burned by multiple agencies, tried hiring in-house, realized they didn't know enough to lead/train/hold people accountable — so they want to learn and be led themselves. Inner Circle fits that exactly.
- Jeremy AI sells because owners now "want an AI model to talk to instead of having to watch a course" — agentic, connectable, real-time answers vs. waiting 10–12 hours for a human reply.

### 2.5 Sell to rich people only
- "I don't sell to poor people. I help rich people get richer. That is literally my job." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue]
- Target demographic in every video: businesses doing a couple hundred grand/month to a couple million/month. Lower-tier viewers still pay attention but are not the buyers. (He serves poorer audiences through free content and his charity, the Haynes Family Foundation — not through the business.)

### 2.6 Marketing engine behind the MRR
- **Organic (YouTube):** the long-term play. Sales cycle of ~3–6 months before a viewer buys — typically after they try something he taught, get a result, then spend that "house money" with him. He began twice-a-week YouTube in June 2024 as a ~90-day test of his old thesis that content wouldn't produce clients; it produced education-company demand (not agency clients).
- **Paid ads:** a "no-brainer," used differently from organic. Mechanisms that work for the transactional/AI offers: **webinars** ("one of the best content mechanisms right now in direct response advertising") and **lead magnets** (one new lead magnet produced per day by a dedicated hire). An AI agent in the CRM then contextually follows up with free value to move leads toward a sale. **Call funnels do NOT work well** for Inner Circle (that stays an organic long-term play); ads work better for transactional offers (Master Internet Marketing) and Jeremy AI.
- **Networking / word of mouth** is a real moat: "We trust pods of people who already trust one another." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue] Nearly every Inner Circle deal has a word-of-mouth influence; almost no one buys fully isolated. Getting in front of already-trusted networks (e.g., going on Ryan Cloag's channel) expedites trust via authority bias.

### 2.7 Cost structure of the recurring business
He runs a sizeable team to generate the MRR: **~$213,000–$286,000/month on team.** Roles named: fractional CFO; lawyer (also tax); in-house IG guy ($10k/mo, started at $8k); CTO + tech CSM/developer (Jeremy AI); a CSM (Vic) to hold Jeremy AI users' hands; two marketing helpers at different levels (one ~$20k+/mo for YouTube clips on The Haynes Feed, one dedicated to one lead magnet per day); in-person executive assistant (~$8k/mo all-in); in-person full-time personal trainer (justified as revenue-driven — fitness converts buyers); driver; YouTube editing team ($8.5k/mo); **sales team — the biggest variable cost at ~$80,000–$150,000/month** (individual salespeople make $80k–$150k/month); part-time blog agency ($5k/mo); remote EA for low-level work. Note: this payroll covers ONLY the recurring-revenue component; total org revenue (incl. transactional) is not disclosed.

### 2.8 Top lessons (027) and bottom lessons (027)
**Top lessons that got him there:** YouTube (biggest impact); ads (second); gaining leverage to raise prices; the training-data flywheel; offer–narrative alignment; selling to rich people; A-player talent concentration (he once had 27 staff "with a lot of donkeys," now far fewer, better-paid A-players); **morale management** (the NBA-comeback analogy — keep morale high through flat periods); networking/word of mouth.

**Bottom lessons that held him back:** mindset / not thinking big enough / going into autonomous "ride the momentum" slumps (fixed with coaches, a hypnotist, books, network); inconsistency — especially not taking ads/webinars seriously sooner; not hiring enough good people soon enough; **tolerance** — he once went sideways stuck at ~$800k–$850k/month for ~6–7 months and tolerated a "comfortable" version instead of taking significant enough action. His rule: don't tolerate going sideways more than ~2 months in a row; audit your own behavior.

### 2.9 His exaggerated 80/20
He believes it's more extreme than 80/20: "less than 10% of what I do... creates 90 plus percent of my results." [How I Hit $1M/Month In Recurring Revenue] He constantly tries to cut the 90%-effort/10%-result actions and hunt for new 10%-effort/90%-result actions.

## 3. The agency model: $1M/month with ~10 clients (the rev-share game)

### 3.1 The founding math
He burned down his old agency to rebuild around one simple game:
- Old agency: 27 staff, 3 offices (2 Beverly Hills, 1 Miami), took "anybody and everybody," sold every service imaginable, netting roughly $200k–$220k/month.
- The change: "fired 23 of those 27 staff, kept four." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients] Closed all 3 offices, went fully remote, back to Miami.
- The target math: **$1M/month ÷ 10 clients = $100,000/month per deal.** He didn't want to work with more than 10 clients (today willing to do up to 15, due to greater efficiency).
- He was convinced he could get $100k/month per deal via rev-share WITHOUT adding services, effort, or large staff — because he'd already taken 5–8 businesses past $1M/month, so 10% of that felt fair and realistic.

### 3.2 The foundational mindset: results, not time/effort
- "Clients didn't care at all about my time and effort. They solely cared about results." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients]
- Engineer's logic for delivery: "How could we put as little in as humanly possible and still get the level of result that we're looking for and beyond?" [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients] Judge work by result, never by hours spent. This keeps staff lean.

### 3.3 The four services only (nothing else)
Everything is narrowed to what has the highest probability of making the client $1M/month:
1. **Advertising**
2. **Funnels** (only when tied to paid ads — not endless funnel building)
3. **Marketing automation** (email, SMS, Zapier connections)
4. **Consulting** (what to sell, how to price, what to do next, who to hire)

Rule of the game: "We would only do the things that we thought... could benefit this client's probability of making at least a million dollars a month." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients] If a client proposes something that isn't the most revenue-driven action, they say no.

### 3.4 Client selection: client-specific, not niche-specific
- "It is not niche specific... It is client specific." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients]
- He uses **23 "perfect client traits."** They look for **23/23**; rarely make exceptions. Occasionally take 19/23 if there's upside, but exceptions "typically doesn't play out in our favor."
- Typical starting client revenue: **$100k–$300k/month**, and they desperately want to reach $1M/month (that's their next income goal).
- Prefer **high-profit-margin, scalable** offers.
- Track record unlocks bigger deals: having taken **36 businesses past $1M/month** (two reached $5M and $5.5M/month) means bigger clients (already at $1M–$2M/month) will hire him to go to $5M because he's "been there, done that."

### 3.5 The closing/filter sequence (in order)
1. Confirm perfect client traits (aim 23/23) and that they're in the right income range.
2. They must **agree to the pricing structure** (fee + rev share).
3. **Set expectations properly** — not every qualified client will reach $1M/month (sales team can implode, service may not scale, a key person leaves, or they "check out" after hitting a big-but-smaller number).
4. **Financially model the deal** (see §3.6) — then **revisit expectations AND pricing a second time** using the model. Only after both are agreed twice do they close and onboard.

### 3.6 The financial model (call-funnel example, verbatim math from 039)
Reverse-engineering from a $1M/month goal with a $5,000 offer:
- $1,000,000 ÷ $5,000 = **200 sales/month** needed.
- At a **20% close rate** → **1,000 calls** needed.
- At a **60% show rate** → ~**1,667 bookings** needed.
- At **$150 cost per call** → 1,667 × $150 ≈ **$28,375 ad spend**. (Note: the transcript narration mixes "$125" and "$150" cost-per-call phrasing; the worked number he lands on is based on $150.)
- This rigid math assumes the full $5,000 is cash-collected (no payment plans).

Then stress-test against reality:
- Ask real **AOV / cash collected** (e.g., only $3,800 collected vs. $5,000 charged changes everything).
- Vary show rate, close rate, margin. At low cash-collect + low close, required spend can balloon (he cites a possible ~$400,000) vs. ~$125,000 in a strong scenario.
- Build the full P&L the client will actually net: ad spend + sales commission (e.g., 20% of $1M = $200k) + business delivery expenses + your $100k fee + taxes. In his example, ad spend ($28k) + commission ($200k) + his fee ($100k) alone ≈ $328k — "that's half... gone" before other expenses and tax, so a client netting a "million" may keep far less than they imagine. This is why margin matters (he cites a client at only 20% margin — a $100k sale nets just $20k).

Purpose of the model: establish **probabilities + ethics + proper expectations** in real time, on a shared screen, so both sides agree the deal is fair.

### 3.7 Rules of the game after closing
- **Only do the highest-revenue-probability actions, in order, top of list down.** Do nothing else.
- Example first moves: launch a new call funnel with updated VSL, get in-market direct-response ads shot, run a mini content-sequence ad strategy.
- "We do not listen to the client when they propose things... that are not the most revenue-driven thing to do." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients]

### 3.8 Speed
Because of the laser focus and defined game, he's taken clients to $1M/month "within 60 to 90 days." Operating inside a defined game dramatically increases speed of achievement.

## 4. Rev-share deal structure — the canonical terms

> His modern, standing structure (stated identically in both 039 and 054).

### 4.1 The base template
- **Fee: $15,000/month** (recurring, every 30 days).
- **Rev share: 10% net.**
- **Goal per deal: $100,000/month** — which requires netting the client **~$1,000,000/month** trackable to his work.
- "Nine and a half out of 10 deals we do are this structure." [The CORRECT Way To Do Revenue Share Deals]

### 4.2 The "either/or" fee-vs-revshare mechanic
The fee is **deducted from** the rev share owed — the client effectively pays only the higher of the two:
- If client nets **≤ $150k** (trackable): they pay **only the $15k fee** (because $150k × 10% = $15k).
- If client nets **> $150k**: they pay the $15k fee **plus the difference** up to the full 10%.
- Worked example: client nets $200k → owes $20k rev share → they already paid $15k fee → they wire the **$5k difference**. Net effect: "it's framed like an either-or thing," but the $15k is always charged, then deducted from rev share owed.

### 4.3 Why charge an upfront fee at all — skin in the game
- Pure rev-share (no fee) does NOT work: "when we charge this 15K a month, we work a lot harder upfront and it... enables us to prioritize the deal." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients]
- Without skin in the game, clients move like "little turtles" — slow logins, slow ad shoots, slow VSLs — "they don't need to do things on their end fast." The fee makes the client act fast.
- How to size YOUR fee: pick a number that "makes you feel like it's worth it" — not too little, not too much. $15k for him: meaningful enough to prioritize the deal, but low enough that he can refund and fire a bad client without it hurting, yet not so low he'd fire them frivolously.

### 4.4 What "net" means (what may/may not be deducted)
"Net" = literal net. Allowed deductions against revenue trackable to him:
- Ad spend
- Sales commissions
- Cost of goods sold / service costs / hard product costs
- (Some savvy clients) projected monthly tax burden

**NOT allowed:** expenses that are not hard costs on the money generated — e.g., using the revenue to buy another business, over-hiring, or randomly staffing unrelated departments and "justifying that like it comes against" your revenue. Know the normal expense range before entering (typically ~30–50%, higher for e-com — "upwards of 70 to 80%... at scale") so you can spot a client trying to "dick you out of money."

### 4.5 Only revenue TRACKABLE back to you
- "We're taking revshare off of what is trackable back to us... our funnel, our ads, our marketing. That's it." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients] Never off the client's entire organization revenue, and never off what they were already making — only new revenue his work brings in.

### 4.6 Billing mechanics
- Rev shares calculated/collected **between the 1st and 5th of the following month.**
- Calculation handled by the client's bookkeeper/accountant with his bookkeeper (he keeps a bookkeeper for ~a couple hundred $/month; will staff one onto a client who lacks one).
- Payment plans / later-collected cash: if most cash is collected upfront, he keeps it simple and gets paid off front-end cash collected (still only what's trackable). For low-ticket recurring or heavy payment-plan businesses, he defines getting paid on that revenue on an ongoing basis as it's collected.

### 4.7 Jeremy AI member case: a $10M/year AI-first business (same agency logic, client side)
Inner Circle member "Tom" built a business selling hyperbaric chambers (~$50k+ AOV) from $0 to $1M+/month (~$10M+ over the year) with **zero full-time employees** — only a few one-and-done contractors. Relevant to the lean/AI-leveraged model:
- Flow: Facebook/Meta **instant (lead) form** → **AI setter** (qualifies + raises purchase intent) → **three-way text intro to Tom**, who closes. "Tom's just generating himself layups all day." [How He Built A $10M/Year Business With Zero Employees Using AI]
- Stack: Claude (Opus 4.6) as the brain — "In Tom's words, Claude is everything." [How He Built A $10M/Year Business With Zero Employees Using AI] Voice via Eleven Labs (Jeremy preferred Bland AI for lower latency). Texting via Send Blue (tested Blue Bubbles). Training data/SOPs/"war room" in Notion; tool access to Salesforce (CRM), QuickBooks (quotes/invoices/cash-collection), Asana (agent task management).
- The AI setter does far more than financial qualification: answers all product/pricing/delivery questions, references real-time inventory and manufacturing ETAs, writes personalized email sequences.
- Takeaway for the firm's economics: an AI-first delivery layer can run a $1M+/month operation with near-zero headcount — the extreme end of Jeremy's "results not effort / stay lean" principle.

## 5. When he does NOT charge a rev share (exceptions & creative structures)

Fewer than ~2–5% of deals. Three scenarios where a qualified client resists the rev share:

**Exception 1 — Hard-flex / case-study trade (only for category kings).**
Only if the client is "massive and excessively well-known," the top dog everyone in the niche knows. Then:
- Charge a flat fee **higher** than normal — "maybe like 25 or 30k a month." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients]
- In exchange, the client must let you "hard flex" — content, website, sales calls, video pitches, reference calls.
- This is offered BY the agency, not negotiated down by the client.

**Exception 2 — Capped rev share.**
Real example: a client did $7M off one organic webinar and balked at a 10% rev share (~$700k). Solution: keep 15k/mo + 10% net but **cap the rev share at $100k/month**. "That way we hit our goal... and you... make much more than that." The client was happy.

**Exception 3 — Tiered / incremental pricing.**
Real example: a New York real-estate personal brand wouldn't do a rev share AND didn't want the deal talked about. Solution: incremental flat fees tied to revenue milestones — $15k → $30k → $45k → $60k, stepped at revenue milestones (first at ~$200k, then increments of ~$200k: 400k, 600k, 800k). "In our head it was still a rev share, just a little different." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients]

**The three fallbacks when a client resists pricing:** (1) tiered pricing; (2) the hard-flex trade (only if they're the top dog); or (3) **be willing to walk away.** "We do not capitulate on those deals."

## 6. The RISKS of the rev-share model (and how to handle them)

### 6.1 Client sells the business (no equity = you get rug-pulled)
- Happened 3 times, each a private equity buyout. Because he had only fee + rev share and no equity, he becomes "their biggest expense by far" and gets cut at acquisition.
- PE firms are "essentially just marketing agencies with a bunch of cash" who think they can run it better — and in all 3 cases ran it into the ground (one went from $1.7M/month to $40k/month; another from $2M/month to ~$300–700k/month).
- **Fix — "provisional equity" clause:** he does NOT take equity upfront (too much friction). Instead: "if and when you get bought out and we've contributed more than this dollar amount trackable back to us, then you give us 10% provisional equity." [How I'm Scaling My Agency To $1M/Month With 10 Clients] Worded to trigger when a serious exit process begins (not only at the literal closing), "that way... we don't get [screwed]."
- Opportunity: 2 of 3 bought-out clients later rehired him when the PE firm destroyed the business.

### 6.2 The friend / mastermind who seeds doubt
- A peer tells the client they're crazy to pay $100k/month ("you could pay 10 people 10K a month"). He cites "a CIA document... how to sabotage an organization" — just **seed doubt**; it grows over time.
- Once growth plateaus and the big numbers feel "normal," the client wrongly believes they can remove you and revenue will hold. They'll fire you. No contract wording fully prevents this: "If they want you out, they're going to find a way to get you out."
- **Fix — keep the line of communication open; no ego.** When they fire you and then contract hard, reach out ("just checking in"), offer to take a look, and re-engage. "Don't let the ego prevent money."
- **Re-engagement pricing:** if YOU reach out, charge what you charged before; if THEY reach out, "you charge the [expletive] tax" (more than before) — you have all the leverage.

### 6.3 A competitor with similar authority undercuts you
- Someone at or near your level offers to do it cheaper, usually starting with an "audit," then quietly getting added to the account (watch for signs: new person/agency added, client asking for your SOPs or a daily log).
- **Fix — call it out directly and transparently.** Tell the client they're risking a lot; if they try and fail and come back, you'll charge more; and their move makes you want to "clam up" and deprioritize them. Create counter-leverage: "we're going to go pitch every single competitor that they have" and line up sales calls to replace the client by month-end. Of 5–6 such confrontations, he's walked ~twice and made MORE from the competitors' deals, while the original client lost money. Reason: it's harder than it looks to maintain revenue without knowing what got it there — "you start the iteration cycle again."

### 6.4 Rev share can take months to pay out (expectation risk)
- "Don't think that the rev share model is a get-rich-quick model... This takes work." [The CORRECT Way To Do Revenue Share Deals]
- Below $150k net, you only earn the fee. A $100–300k/month client may not net $150k until you push them to $400–500k. Big rev-share payouts often start month 3–6, not month 1 (month-1/2 payouts happen but are rare). Scaling exposes operational/sales-team/sales-cycle problems that take time to fix.

### 6.5 Avoid "fly-by-night" / get-rich-quick niches (legal & clawback liability)
Two red-flag characteristics that attract bad operators like a magnet:
1. The operator **doesn't have to do much fulfillment**, AND
2. The product is **high ticket**.
(These can look like green flags — efficient, high-margin, low-fulfillment — but attract unethical people who start "with as little work as humanly possible after they sell.") Risks: FTC/SEC exposure, civil lawsuits from failed customers, and — when the business files bankruptcy — **clawback**: a court going down the vendor sheet and, because you were paid a lot on rev share, flagging you as an "unjustifiable expense" that must pay money back. Only work with operators who are the best at fulfillment and making good on promises.

### 6.6 Client dilution (concentration risk)
- When one rev-share deal becomes huge, it can be 70–80% of your agency revenue — lose it and you lose most of your income overnight.
- **Target: no single rev-share client should exceed ~20% of total revenue.** 20% beats 70%, and you lower it further by closing more deals. Aim for revenue evenly segmented across several substantial deals (not one whale + a stack of "baby fee deals").

### 6.7 Bad sales teams (biggest silent money-loser)
- A great marketing engine still fails if the client's sales team "shits the bed" and the client won't fire fast.
- **Fix — secretly shop the client's sales team BEFORE the deal:** test show-rate sequences, manual follow-up, close rate. A client unwilling to fire/rehire fast is a red flag.

## 7. Green flags / red flags & the real-time lessons habit

- **Green flags = his "perfect client traits"** (23 traits; characteristics, niches, prices, funnel strategies that work). When he spots a working pattern, he tells his sales team to "drop everything and start pitching this type of person."
- **Red flags** get projected forward: assume a bad pattern will repeat and move away from it.
- Marketing feedback principle applied to deals: if one lead/client reveals a lesson, "there's probably a thousand other people that experienced it" — act on single data points fast. "We'll just assume that it's going to be like that for every other person moving forward."

## 8. The right questions to ask before entering a rev-share deal
Three categories (asked efficiently — his sales calls are ~45–60 min, usually a two-call process):
1. **Expectation questions** — the "if this, then that" scenarios (e.g., the mastermind-doubt scenario), verbally walked through before the deal.
2. **Math questions** (feed a financial model): current cost per call; qualified-vs-unqualified application ratio; show rate; setter/closer model; close rate; **cash collected** (not revenue); salesperson commission; business expenses. Plug in → net out the other side → proper, shared expectations.
3. **Ethics/integrity questions** — ask their current expense ratio and whether it fluctuates (rarely >~10%); if 40%, treat 30–50% as the ethical range; probe any claim that expenses would balloon at scale. Vet every business for morals: "You want to work with people have ethics... people have high morals... that are integrous."

## 10. Relationship & staff management (what sustains the recurring money)

### 10.1 Client relationships
- Anyone paying $100k+/month wants a genuine relationship: be available, do the little things. He cites tracking clients' kids' birthdays, buying gifts, calling to talk, and donating ~$5k to a client's kid's fundraiser — "because I actually give a [expletive]... about the people that are paying me this amount of money." [The CORRECT Way To Do Revenue Share Deals]
- Play the long game, not short-term. Don't get talked down on price; "stick to your guns... know what you're worth." As you get rich from it, stay grounded and stay in the trenches — don't check out.

### 10.2 Staff incentives (don't be greedy)
- "Staff needs incentives... You can't be pocketing [$100k]/month per deal and not paying your staff extremely well." [The CORRECT Way To Do Revenue Share Deals]
- A players should get **a small percentage of the upside** (down to EAs) so everyone moves up as the org grows and has reason to rebuild when it contracts.
- Also: team retreats, more coaching, proper medical benefits, bonuses for one-off contractors.
- Result: since starting rev share, client relationships last multiple years and staff have stayed 6–10 years. "It really is the long game in its finest form."

### 10.3 Three things that make the rev-share model easier (caveats, 054)
