# Scaling to $1M/Month — Jeremy Haynes' Roadmap & Framework

> Source: Jeremy Haynes' own YouTube transcripts (4 videos — see **Source videos** at the end). Everything here is grounded in what he actually says in those videos. Jeremy teaches scaling high-ticket coaching / info / agency / service / B2B businesses to $1M+/month via paid ads (mainly Meta), VSL & call funnels, offers, and sales.
>
> **Important framing:** Jeremy does NOT teach a neat "$0 → $100K → $250K → $500K → $1M" stage-gate. His organizing principle is **the math** — it "reveals the future" and tells you what to do next from wherever you are. The staged section below is reconstructed from the specific bottlenecks he diagnoses in his case studies and live breakdowns, with each claim tied to its source. Where the transcripts give a benchmark or number, it's cited; where a stage is inferred from a case study, that's stated.

---

## 1. The master sequence: the order of operations

Jeremy's repeated top-down sequence for cracking "a million dollars a month" (or the *next* million) runs in this order [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]:

1. **The Math** (consideration math) — reveals whether what you're doing now even works.
2. **The Offer** — what you sell to make the math possible. *The offer precedes everything else.*
3. **Traffic source** — chosen by the momentum you can immediately capitalize on.
4. **Funnel / conversion mechanism** — chosen by your **sales team's** strengths.
5. **Closer Math** — reverse-engineer calls, closers, ad spend, fulfillment load.
6. **The Action Cycle** — launch → get data → fix → scale; solve clues fast.
7. **Team & Systems** — offer/channel owners, documentation, SOPs (surfaces most at $1M+).

Signature line on why math comes first:
> "The math makes you capable of knowing when to do what and knowing how far away you are from what you actually claim you want to achieve." [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

Critical ordering caveat — the offer outranks the traffic+funnel combo:
> "the offer specifically precedes it because these things will not matter and they will be very high friction if the offer sucks." [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

## 2. Step 1 — The Math ("math reveals the future")

Three variables, and you start from the revenue goal:

- **(1) What people pay you** — "the first and most important variable of all... the starting point."
- **(2) Quantity of clients** you need/want to work with.
- **(3) The revenue goal** — $1M/month (specify *paid to you* each month, recurring vs transactional).

Two ways to solve the same equation:
- `$1,000,000 ÷ (clients you want) = what you must charge`
- `$1,000,000 ÷ (what people currently pay you) = how many clients you need`

**Worked examples he gives:**

| Business | Goal | Decision | Resulting math |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing agency (8 yrs ago) | $1M/mo | Only wanted **10 clients** | Each must pay **$100K/mo** (10 × 100K = 1M) |
| Jeremy AI (scalable offer) | $1M/mo recurring | Charge **$1,000/mo** (low enough for volume, high enough to avoid wrong demo) | Need **1,000 clients**. Currently ~$194K/mo = 19% to goal; launched March |

Key lesson from running the math at the agency: dividing $1M by what clients *currently* paid gave "an ungodly amount of clients... much more than I would want the responsibility or load to bear." That instantly told him the model was wrong and the number had to come down → change the offer.

**Nimbleness rule:**
> "One thing that I see people do time and time again is they are very static and rigid with whatever offer they currently have... We are very nimble with our offers... The math is what determines what we do." [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

## 3. Step 2 — The Offer (precedes everything)

The offer is "what specifically was I going to do to make that math possible."

**Agency offer redesign (his real example):**
- Dropped the **flat retainer** → it didn't fit the math.
- Moved to **$10,000/mo fee + 10% net profit share** (he considered gross-revenue or profit share, settled on *net* profit share).
- Only on **trackable** revenue — "revenue that was actually trackable. You could point your finger at it and say that originated from these guys." Fair and agreeable.
- **Month-to-month, no contracts**: "if a client didn't want to be in the deal, I likely wouldn't want to be in the deal either."
- Required a **richer client** who could spend a lot on ad spend fast → higher probability of getting paid in a shorter timeline.

**Offer timeline he reports (agency):**
- ~**3.5 months** from doing the math → changing the offer → landing the first client under the new structure.
- ~**4.5–5 months** to get that client paying **over $100K/mo**.
- **8 months** (from landing) to get them **over $200K/mo**.
- After that: "a simple game. Pursue more clients that could actively pay that amount of money."

**Scalable-offer logic (Jeremy AI):** more clients = better, so charge much less ($1,000/mo) but not so low you attract "poor people or the wrong demographic."

## 4. Step 3 — Traffic source (chosen by momentum)

> "How you go about exploiting one [traffic source] over the other is directly determined by the amount of momentum that you can immediately capitalize on and capture." [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

- Brand-new account on YouTube/IG = "likely going to be a dog traffic source for you."
- If you already have momentum on an organic channel, that can **triumph testing paid ads** to start validating the math — "it made more sense to do what we were already doing that already had momentum."
- If you must go new, "educate yourself extensively... on what the best practices are of that traffic channel."

**His real traffic sources:**
- **Agency:** "perfect cold video pitches" — a custom video per prospect proposing a unique strategy, + **weekly value-driven follow-up**. He *headhunts / snipes* ideal clients; "we never really get our ideal client... through paid advertising" (only 3× in 2+ years on the channel).
- **Jeremy AI:** organic **Instagram** (posts ~8×/day with CTAs to comment → delivered via **MiniChat** DMs — free lead magnets, the **"scaling plan"** trial), **YouTube** as a conversion mechanism, and **paid** traffic pointed at the scaling plan. A **setter** (Benji) works the leads onto closers' calendars.

**Paid (FB/IG) conversion path he runs:** intro (who he is + income + how many clients pay him) → invites qualified business types to **trial the clone** → free **scaling plan** (answer ~20 questions; "the depth of the answers... directly determine the output") → show sample scaling plans + testimonials → "it's free, fill out this application" → emailed over.

## 5. Step 4 — Funnel / conversion (chosen by the SALES TEAM)

> "Your sales team's strengths and weaknesses are what actively determine what funnels are even available to you to use." [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

- If your reps only close **"layup deals"** from warm organic traffic, don't assume a cold call funnel from paid will work — "your call funnel would [not] still work."
- Pick a funnel that recreates the *type* of deal the team already handles.
- **Kian example:** traffic = **cold calling** (~65,000 calls/mo); funnel = closer call off the cold call; team = "monsters... killers." For that team, bias toward **high volume, low cost-per-lead**. The same advice would make an Instagram-DM-organic seller "flop miserably."

This "traffic source + funnel" pairing is "the most important combo" — but the **offer still precedes it**.

## 6. Step 5 — Closer Math (reverse-engineering the machine)

Distinct from consideration math. Reverse-engineer from the revenue goal down to daily ad spend and headcount. His worked example:

```
$1,000,000 goal
÷ AOV $5,000 (cash collected per sale)      = 200 closes needed
÷ 20% close rate (of shown calls)           = 1,000 shown calls
÷ 60% show rate                             = 1,667 booked calls
× $150 cost per call                        = $250,050 ad spend / month
                                            ≈ $8,335 / day
```

Then capacity / headcount:
- `1,667 booked calls ÷ 20 workdays/mo ≈ 83 calls/day`
- `83 ÷ 8 calls per closer/day ≈ 10 closers`

**Caveats he flags on closer capacity:** 8 calls/day is not realistic to assume as pure load — reps "got to eat... go to the bathroom... follow up with the people that they talked to previously. Otherwise, you'll create a **buy or die culture** where none of your leads get followed up with" and CRM compliance dies. The 60% no-show slack is the follow-up/CRM time.

**What the math buys you (clarity):**
- Total ad spend + spend/day (and whether your account's spend limits even allow it — e.g. $500/day vs $8,335/day shows how far off you are).
- How many closers you need vs have.
- Fulfillment load (200 closes/mo → what does delivery look like).
- Whether the current traffic channel / funnel "can even be scaled to that level before it breaks."
> "Other people mathematically calculate and therefore can see the exact problems they're going to experience and when they are probable to experience them." [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

## 7. Step 6 — The Action Cycle (island / treasure / clues)

Metaphor he teaches Inner Circle students: you wash up on an island, treasure exists, you get one clue that leads to the next clue. "Forward progress solely comes down to how fast we can problem solve getting from one clue to the next."

- Some clues are "screamingly obvious," others are hidden side-quests.
- "The speed in which you solve problems determines the speed in which you scale."
- Getting **stuck** in the middle (the jungle) is where the risk/"death" is — get across fast.
- Launch something → it gives data on what to fix → once it's "a nice thing," **label it as scalable** and amplify to find out if it actually scales. Expect to change/drop/add things; "not everything is as clean as just get something launched and then amplify the hell out of it."

**The two problems everything reduces to** (the "bicycle"):
1. **Filling up your closers' calendars.**
2. **Getting more closers.**
> "Every problem that we've seen time and time again is literally a subpros of one of those two key areas." Move rapidly back and forth between the two. [*If I Wanted to Hit $1M/Month in 2026*]

## 8. Reconstructed stage view (grounded in the case studies)

Jeremy doesn't label fixed revenue stages, but each case study/breakdown exposes the **bottleneck that shows up at a given level**. This maps the real diagnoses:

### Stage A — Brand new / $0 ("the sea turtle")
- A brand-new owner is a baby sea turtle sprinting to the ocean past sharks and birds — "the highest probability of death." Paid *or* organic, "their stats are against them either way." What matters is the **trait of the individual** (endurance, a little financial backing) and having a mentor/perspective. [*1 Business Coach vs 10 Business Owners*]
- **Prove the offer fast:** paid is the fastest way — a couple hundred dollars beats "eight months" growing an organic page that gets ~800 views and doesn't reach your demo. Organic isn't free (time has a cost).
- **Newbie gains:** platforms (Meta) are built to give first-time spenders their highest probability of a return — "their way of activating a customer." So beginners often *do* get early returns on paid. But **match risk to finances** — don't spend your last dollars (casino rule: only gamble what you're happy to vaporize).
- **Work in multiples of 100** — get ≥100 people to do something (e.g. click to a page) before making page changes or pivoting. "Around that threshold of 100... you can start being more deterministic."
- **Sales vs fulfillment:** "you're not a business if you don't have sales." Put the *majority* of attention on sales early, a minority on fulfillment; shift the ratio once deals land. You can white-label fulfillment (charge $3K, pay a $1.5–2K partner, pocket the difference) and pre-sell before a product exists. [*1 Business Coach vs 10 Business Owners*]

### Stage B — Small agency/service, stuck at low 5-figures ($15–35K/mo)
- Diagnosed live with "Derek" (15–30K/mo) and "Dan" (~$3,500 avg ticket). Core fixes:
  - **Raise prices / sell to richer clients.** Charge *relative to the outcome you can produce*. Higher price → you deliver harder, clients take it seriously (the "fruits & vegetables vs A5 wagyu" and "$500 = half don't log in; $5K = 100% log in" analogies). Low pricing often signals "insecurity in your capacity to deliver."
  - **"Pimping a deal":** take a small deal, make it *bigger*, which unlocks slightly bigger clients; repeat — "every next deal... becomes a deal that can be bigger because they're in a better position to start." You *must* start small and get 1–2 deals bigger to unlock the next market tier. Don't stay anchored to small clients who cost you time. [*1 Business Coach vs 10 Business Owners*]
  - Bias toward your **"perfect client traits"** (he vets ~20; "we say no way more than we say yes").

### Stage C — Plateau at $300–400K/mo → the NRR unlock
- **Daniel Fazio** (Cold Email Wizard) was "stuck at like 300 or 400k a month" from mid-2023 to early-2024. The unlock was **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**, which he calls "a metric that is 30 times more important than churn." [*Asking Business Owners How They Scaled to $1M/Month*]
  - `NRR = (what you start with + upsells − churn) ÷ what you started with`
  - **<100% NRR = the business atrophies and dies.** 80% NRR with no new customers = "dead in 5 months."
  - HubSpot went from 80% → ~110–115% NRR by having "hella to buy" (14+ subscriptions under one account).
  - Fix: **"just launch more for people to buy"**; make it hard to fully leave; dedicated **"NR boosters"** = a sales team doing outbound email/dialing *to existing customers* (7,000 of them).
  - How his first $1M/mo month actually computed: ~$200K of it was **upsells/cross-sells**; only **194K ad spend**; front-end ROAS was ~**1.3x**, not 5x — because the prior 6 months spent ~$1M on ads to build the customer base to upsell into. ROAS on old campaigns climbs to 5–6x over time (seen in Hyros).

### Stage D — $0→$500K/mo on organic → add paid to reach $1M
- **Brez** got $0→$500K/mo "without touching any paid ads." "Content = connection → conversion." [*Asking Business Owners How They Scaled to $1M/Month*]
  - **Clipping engine:** a clipping community (~43,000 members, ~2,000 active clippers), ~$50K/mo on clippers; "hundreds of clips posted per day" = a "modern-day brand awareness campaign" spreading millions–billions of views; recycled to main pages → conversion.
  - **Paid supplements organic** to push past $500K toward $1M (not either/or).
- General Haynes rule: **paid converts better when organic content exists on every platform** (long + short form). [*1 Business Coach vs 10 Business Owners*, *…How They Scaled*]

### Stage E — ~$1M/mo where "everything breaks"
- **Ryan (Warrior)** — largest case ($30M/yr info/coaching, $80M+ total cash). Going from ~$10M to $30M, "**everything broke.** We were not tracking the numbers. Everything was going up and to the right. But CPA is rising... costs going through the roof... stacked up to over 100 team, moved everyone W2... margin as a percentage dove, but as dollars went up." Then they had to **clean it up**. [*Asking Business Owners How They Scaled to $1M/Month*]
  - **Roles:** expert + marketer + operator (can be one person; became three).
  - **Offer evolution:** $27/mo app ("those economics do not work... it's a whole LTV game, an ascension game") → **call funnels** → **high-ticket 1:1 recurring coaching** (~$5–6K). Reps paid on cash → incentivized to sell high ticket; **"burn the boats"** (2-week window, then shut info down).
  - **One funnel, hammered:** the classic **VSL opt-in funnel did $72M** before they launched another — "we hammered it to the level that the CPA was rising and we kept hammering it." Next iteration = **multi-lead, multi-buyer funnel**; low ticket added recently.

### Stage F — $1M+/mo: the next million comes from the BACK END
- **Brandon** live breakdown ($1.4M USD/mo, wants $5M/mo). His business was "**100% essentially a transactional business**" — only ~$60K/mo USD recurring out of $1.4M. [*What This $1.4M/Mo Business Should Do Next To Scale*]
  - **"Second money is the easiest money"** (his first mentor's quote) — recurring/back-end is "one of the most undermined opportunities."
  - **Cash velocity:** Brandon set himself a **6-month** cycle to "second money"; Jeremy gives himself **30 days** → an entirely different system to get results sooner. "You created this entire game... you are the game maker." Collapse the cycle.
  - **Milestone-based unlocks, not time-based:** reward *achievement* (unlock higher-priced, more advanced content when a client hits a milestone), never "6 months passed" (which just trains everyone to coast for 6 months). Pick the milestone carefully — peg it too low and you won't attract higher-level clients. Jeremy's own system: **$1M / $3M / $5M-a-month trophies** + an exclusive million-earners group chat.
  - **Offer / channel owners:** at $1M+ he recommends internal **"marketing helpers"** who each own ONE funnel or traffic channel full-time (run ads, write copy, use AI). ~$10–20K/mo payroll + **~2.5% of that funnel's revenue** as bonus. "You don't want to offload the responsibility onto one person for many things." Brandon's whole marketing dept was two people treating a $1.4M/mo funnel as "a spare time activity" — unacceptable.
  - **Add true back-end offers:** he had nothing above $450/mo. Recommended: (1) a second, **more intimate higher-priced workshop** (the Mozi **triple-tier workshop** model: large staff-taught entry workshop → mid guest-appearance → most-costly intimate founder-led); (2) a **1:1 / coaching package**; (3) **upsell at the workshop itself** (~80 attendees each). The math: ~750 deals/quarter ≈ **3,000 new customers/year** → 10% × 3,000 = **300 paying +$20K**; 5% = **150 into a higher-tier inner circle**.
  - **Get-richer training** (see §10).

### Stage G — $1M–$1.9M/mo but no profit: "pick a lane"
- **Chris Walker (Refine Labs)** peaked at **$1.9M/mo revenue, almost no profit** (125 people, heavy fixed costs). 2022 "SaaS winter": clients cut marketing first, broke annual agreements → massive churn; **lost $1.6M in 2022** on ~$21M revenue (~$1M in severances, 75 laid off, −$200K/mo). [*Asking Business Owners How They Scaled to $1M/Month*]
  - Jeremy's advice that turned it around (Aug 2023): *"You got a $15 million business. Why are you going to burn that down... You got to pick a lane — you either have to **cash flow it** or **build it to sell it.** You're in the middle right now and doing neither."*
  - Outcome: put his business partner in charge (granted +16.5% equity → 26.5%, 3-year build-to-sell plan, 8-figure exit incentive); refinanced debt **$180K/mo → $24K/mo**; now ~$12.4M/yr (~$1M/mo) with **45 FTE (down from 125)**.
  - Lessons: **cash flow ≠ enterprise value**; agencies are valued off **EBITDA**, not revenue like SaaS; **"profit is better than revenue"**; "a million dollars a month means you're making **25–50% net income margin**"; **"scaling fixed headcount is a death wish"**; "if I could do it all over again, I'd figure out how to get to a million dollars a month with **less than 10 people.**"

## 10. Recurring "if I were you, I'd do X" prescriptions

- **"Do the math first."** Back into everything — spend, closers, fulfillment — so problems become "exactly what you know you're going to be up against next" instead of chaos. [*$1M in 2026*]
- **Fix the offer before touching traffic/funnel.** A bad offer makes everything high-friction. [*$1M in 2026*]
- **Charge for outcomes; sell to rich people.** Rich clients implement faster, need less support, get better results, pay multiples more, higher profit even at higher CPL. [*10 Business Owners*]
- **Raise prices to force better delivery & seriousness** (the $500 vs $5K log-in gap). [*10 Business Owners*]
- **"Pimp the deal"** up the client ladder; don't anchor to small clients. [*10 Business Owners*]
- **Don't stay in test mode too long** — the moment you hit target profitability, "push scale... so I can actually start making some real money." [*10 Business Owners*]
- **Add a back-end / upsell.** To a low-ticket seller: "you'd crack a million a month if you just started upselling... something 5 to 10 grand." Build NRR by having lots to buy. [*10 Business Owners*, *…How They Scaled*]
- **Collapse your cash-velocity cycle** (6 months → 30 days); unlock on **milestones**, not time. [*$1.4M/Mo*]
- **Install funnel/channel owners** at $1M+ (don't run a $1.4M/mo funnel "in your spare time"). [*$1.4M/Mo*]
- **"Pick a lane"** at scale: cash-flow it OR build to sell — never both-and-neither. Profit > revenue. [*…How They Scaled*]
- **Build systems to remove friction** and scale ("without systems you can't scale"; Slack enrollers channel, frictionless payment→agreement→onboarding→video flow; hit stickiness within the first 20–30 min buyer's-remorse window). [*…How They Scaled*]
- **Run organic on every platform to lift paid** — scanner mode (quick judgments) → justification mode (research after booking). Back-end selling systems: **breakout videos** on the confirmation page, **value-dense email sequences**, and the **"hammer them"** retargeting (15–20 pieces of content before the call) to control the frame. [*10 Business Owners*]
- **Get-richer training** (don't let clients plateau and leave): "Don't ever fall under the illusion that you're teaching people Meta and Google ads... you're teaching people how to get rich." Failing to teach it = the "coasting trap," higher churn, lower willingness to pay — "you're actually being unethical by allowing them to leave." [*$1.4M/Mo*]
- **Act on lower certainty** (certainty/burden-of-proof spectrum): top earners move without absolute certainty; certainty-seekers are "always late" and make less. [*10 Business Owners*]
- **Use time constraints** (Parkinson's Law / Musk rocket story) to force efficiency — e.g. review all client ad accounts in <30 min, client calls in 10–15 min. [*10 Business Owners*]

## 11. Evolution, contradictions & nuance to flag

- **Paid-vs-organic is situational, not dogmatic.** In *$1M in 2026* he says to start with whatever traffic has **momentum** (often organic). In *10 Business Owners* he argues **paid is the fastest way to validate an offer**. Not a contradiction — he resolves it by **financial condition & momentum**: brand-new/poor → beware spending last dollars (he grew his own agency via cold video pitches "because I was poor," not ads); if you already have momentum or spare cash, use it. He repeatedly brands himself a **"both" guy, not an "either/or" guy** ("poor people think in either/ors; rich people... do both").
- **Front-end ROAS can be deceptively low (even unprofitable) on purpose** — the real money is back-end/NRR over a 6-month lookback (Daniel's 1.3x front-end → 5–6x later). This sits in tension with Brandon being told his low AOV ($2.5K) makes a call funnel barely viable — reconciled by: low front-end ROAS is fine **only if** you actually have a strong back-end/recurring engine, which Brandon lacked.
- **Revenue vanity vs profit** — he hands out $1M/mo trophies, but explicitly warns (via Chris Walker) that $1M/mo with no margin is a trap; a real $1M/mo should carry 25–50% net margin and ideally <10 people.
- **His own offers evolved and he keeps them nimble** — agency flat retainer → $10K + 10% net profit share; Jeremy AI ($1K/mo) added March; Utari/Nutari (Agentic AI SaaS) still ramping. He treats offers as living things to augment, not fix in stone.
- **Pricing honesty** — he prefers higher ticket / rich buyers for his *own* businesses but says selling to regular/poor people is fine (he does it via clients); it's a preference driven by speed, profit, and "rich person math," plus philanthropy capacity.
- **Universal reductions** — two recurring "everything reduces to" claims: (a) all scaling problems reduce to **filling closer calendars + getting more closers** [*$1M in 2026*]; (b) front-of-funnel psychology reduces to **scanner mode → justification mode** [*10 Business Owners*].
