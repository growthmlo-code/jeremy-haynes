# Mindset & Wealth Psychology — Jeremy Haynes

How the top earners Jeremy Haynes works with actually think, decide, and behave. Everything below is drawn only from six of his YouTube transcripts (listed at the end). Censored profanity in quotes is kept as `[ __ ]` exactly as it appears in the source. Each signature quote carries a [Video Title] citation.

Haynes' self-description of his work frames the whole worldview: "my name is Jeremy Haynes. I help rich people get a hell of a lot richer than they are now." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer] He also splits his teaching into two halves — specific strategy/tactics, and what he calls "get richer talks" — because, in his words, after he gives strategic advice "the person gets in their own way." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer] Mindset is the second half, and he treats it as decisive.

## 1. Money is the lens every decision is actually made through

**Principle: Money — not your stated values — makes nearly every decision in your life, and refusing to admit this keeps you small.**

This is the foundational belief he returns to constantly. The reasoning: a dollar represents value exchanged between people, so your access to money literally determines what you can and can't do, buy, fix, or save.

- "Money is the most important thing because money represents the exchange that we share with one another." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "Everything that you do or that you don't do is 100,000% very clearly, undeniably, inarguably determined by money." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "You think you do you don't your wallet makes your decisions." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

He grounds this with brutal real-world examples rather than theory: a cousin who had to put a dog down over a $5K vet bill she couldn't swing, versus his own ability to clone his dogs for $25–50K; stem-cell IVs for his mother's MS at $7,500 each; a $3,500/year full-body Q-bio MRI scan most people won't buy; his own migraine solved only because he could afford coaches and $30K of recovery equipment. The repeated lesson: "money makes the decision not you money dictates the options you have." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

**Corollary — "feel small, think big" is the strategic position money awareness should produce.** The point of doing the math on money isn't despair, it's a productive feeling of smallness that prevents settling. "You want to feel small and think big. It's the best strategic mental position to maintain." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]

## 2. Stay in reality; judge yourself only by effects, never by your story

**Principle: Reality is a cause-and-effect machine with a delay. Judge yourself exclusively on real-world results, not on the narrative you tell yourself about how hard you're trying.**

He describes a sad pattern among members trying to hit $1M–$5M/month: a minority genuinely try their hardest and get dramatically better results, while most run an internal "story" about effort that reality does not confirm. His diagnostic job is to "poke holes in this story and see if the story and the reality were aligned." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer] Two guests (one at $150–180K/mo stuck 16 months, one at $1.4M/mo) both had elaborate reasons but, in reality, weren't doing the work.

- "Are you genuinely trying as hard as you can?" — his answer from the pattern: about 9 of 10 aren't. [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "You are the cause. You generate the effect. You judge your life and your progress based on the effect." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "If we are not getting the results we want in reality, are we doing the right thing? We can't be." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]

**The lagging-indicator model.** Results are delayed, like a meth addict's face across years of mug shots — today's actions don't show up today, they compound into tomorrow's effect. This is why people stay convinced they're fine while quietly contracting: "we become convinced we're doing better than we are. All we have to do is judge reality." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]

**Practical self-audit he prescribes:** rate honestly 0–100% how hard you've actually been trying, make the number known, and compare yourself to it tomorrow. "You judge reality exclusively. You do not [ __ ] yourself. You do not spin up a false narrative." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]

## 3. Brutal honesty: kill virtue signaling and concealment

**Principle: Lying about your intentions — to others or to yourself — pulls you out of reality, which is the only place progress and money exist.**

He defines virtue signaling as outright lying (e.g., the SaaS founder who claims to "help people" when he actually wants a 40x exit). His objection is operational, not moral: a manipulated internal model can't produce real progress.

- "Virtue signaling is the act of lying it's the act of manipulating your internal model which takes you away from reality." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]
- "Reality is where the action is reality is where the truth is reality is where the money is." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]
- "You define the reasons or the reasons define you." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

**Your driver does not have to be socially acceptable — it just has to be true.** He is emphatic that motivation can be selfish (buy cooler stuff, travel, status) or empathetic (help the underprivileged), and that pretending it must look noble is itself the virtue-signaling trap. "The virtue signaling is thinking it's got to be something that... society is going to accept." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means] He deliberately states things that sound bad out of context (e.g., his split view of homeless people — empathy for families fleeing abuse, none for those he says exploit the system) precisely to demonstrate refusing to perform for societal approval: "I don't virtue signal about what I like or don't like for societal acceptance." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means]

**Don't conceal your ambition.** Concealing that you want to get rich is a form of hiding that costs you — because your money comes from being open with other people. He urges openly declaring large goals (e.g., saying publicly you're going for a billion) rather than collapsing to social pressure or billionaire-criticism. "When I conceal my behavior I'm hiding something." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE] And: "you can't be afraid to get rich you can't conceal it." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

**No LARPing / no fakery.** On the lie detector he confirms he's never bought followers, faked testimonials, lied about an income number, or flexed something he doesn't own, and says of people who do: "I hate when people do that. I think they call it larping... I do not larp." [FBI Examiner Hooked Me To A Lie Detector & Asked Me Brutal Questions] He also candidly admits weaknesses — he could not walk away from his business and be financially fine, he has cried over his business many times — modeling honesty over image.

## 4. The "summiting" model: get bigger than the stuff, then become a sophisticated gambler

**Principle: Success is a mountain with multiple summits. Early on you must rapidly buy and normalize the material "stuff" that is in your way, so it stops occupying your mind and you can evolve into the final mode — the sophisticated gambler.**

He validates this against the "38 Letters from John D. Rockefeller to his Son," noting Rockefeller framed it the same way. [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins] Each summit is a previously-intimidating identity marker (his first was a Y3 wardrobe, then a better apartment, then consistent first class, then cars/homes) that you must get bigger than.

- "you have to feel small, but think massive. Like you have to think huge." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]
- "You're not bigger than a [ __ ] bag... You're not bigger than physical things, like just stuff." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]
- "you should rapidly ascend through the check boxes that are in between." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means]

**Why rush through the stuff:** the material things "will inhibit you until you feel that you've externally created the identity of... whoever you want to be physically." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means] Only once the stuff is out of the way do you unlock the truest money-making version of yourself.

**The sophisticated gambler.** This is his named end-state characteristic of $1M/month earners. It is NOT casino/sports betting — it's taking high-probability calculated bets with businesses, stocks, real estate, even watches as a store of value.

- The game has levels: "The base level of the game is exchanging time for money," then product/service for money, then "putting your money to work to amplify... multiply... compound your money." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "You must learn to become a sophisticated gambler." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- Mountain size dictates chips: "the size of the mountain dictates how many chips you have to gamble with." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]

He frames his own watch-buying during the Swiss-tariff period and a planned $8M house down-payment as "two what I consider sophisticated bets at the same time" — the recurring motive being to "get richer." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means] He also stresses the gambler must be "cool, calm, collected, willing to lose, but it's not going to [ __ ] bankrupt you." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins] Luck is not random: "you create luck and you manufacture luck and you increase the odds that luck plays out in your favor." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]

## 5. Normalize a bigger level; keep the internal world ahead of the external

**Principle: Diminish what you currently consider substantial — deliberately, using hard data and a high-level environment — so a much bigger game feels normal.**

He uses his environment as a conditioning tool: a Miami dealership where his six-figure car was among the cheapest; a neighbor's $450M mega-yacht; ~30 construction cranes visible from his balcony, each a $10M–$200M bet. The purpose is to recalibrate what "a lot of money" means.

- "feel small, think big." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]
- "You have to go out there and look at hard data that... tells you you're a [ __ ] small fry." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]
- "to condition and normalize substantial stuff and bring it back down to just your base level reality." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]

**The internal-vs-external rule (how to never collapse under growth):** You must expand your inner worldview before your external results catch up — because you have time to do it at the lower levels, and you don't want reality to arrive before your mind is ready.

- "the internal world needs to far exceed the external world." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]
- "you do not... want the external world to exceed the internal world. You will feel all kinds of anxiousness." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]

**Don't do it all at once.** Normalization is brick-by-brick: "It's a brickby brick process. It's a stepbystep, daybyday, transaction by transaction, butt in seat action." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]

## 6. Identity is fluid; engineer self-awareness and emotional drivers

**Principle: You are not a static character. Treat your traits, beliefs, and even your physique as adjustable, and build the mental "frames" that serve getting richer.**

He asks directly: "how many mental frames... do you currently have stacked in your favor to help get you richer versus how many... are keeping you anchored." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE] He backs new thoughts with data to convert them into beliefs (e.g., citing a Princeton finding that income keeps raising well-being to argue getting richer makes you happier), and curates environment (money playlists, 40 Sonos speakers) and inputs.

- "your personality type all your characteristics they're fluid you're not a static person." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]
- "if your mind sets off you're off." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

**Self-awareness is the gating skill — most people lack it.** He describes most people as viewing themselves as static "NPCs," unable to watch themselves third-person or change traits on purpose. The "it factor" high achievers seem to have is, in his view, mostly manufactured from self-awareness plus a tapped-in motivational driver, not genetics. "I believe you can have an IT factor if you... have a true level of self-awareness around the driver that you can tap into in a given moment that makes the sacrifice to get the outcome worth it." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means] His own example: pursuing fitness because he knows "I'll get more people to buy... if I get physically fitter," and tapping into rage at a critic ("the German kid") to finish a hard set. The nuance: evoke the emotion/energy, don't act pettily on it.

**Adjust your motivational drivers as you level up.** What drove you to get rich won't carry you from rich to wealthy; you must find new, bigger drivers (generational wealth, scaled impact, proving expanded potential) rather than needing to "go back to broke" to feel the old fire. ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE] Motivation can be a "mystery box" driver too — each new level of money/space reveals "unknown unknowns," new things you didn't even know you could think with, which pulls him to pursue more. [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means]

## 7. Work ethic & focus: highest-revenue actions, intolerance, and manufactured urgency

**Principle: The $1M/month operator relentlessly spends time on the single highest-revenue-driving action available, and ignores the bottom half of the to-do list.**

Advising a member (Josh) who had replaced himself out of his tasks: "All you got to do is do what everybody who makes a million dollars a month does. They focus on the highest revenue driven actions." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner] Whatever you personally focus on, you do best, fastest, and most likely to produce a result — and repetition habitualizes it into neural pathways.

**Principle: Become intolerable of slowness, tolerance, and failure — endure and keep swinging.**

He contrasts stuck operators who try something once, run out of risk money or "bitch out," and revert to safety, with earners who endure. Example: a client ran weekly cold webinars for three months breaking even/losing until every rate was dialed, then hit 4:1 ROAS. His demand on yourself and your team:

- "You've got to become intolerable. No more of this [ __ ] lolly gagging." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]
- "You got to be intolerable to slowness." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]
- On profit-maxing too early at a couple hundred K/mo: "you've also kind of become a [ __ ]... You're not taking big swings anymore." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]

**Principle: A leader's job is to manufacture urgency and a believed mission ("culture").** Humans default to energy conservation (his example, echoing the Snowflake CEO: look at the DMV). So the leader incites urgency and reasons, without creating instability. "Humans naturally fall to the most baseline low level of energy conservation." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner] And: "Culture is a shared set of beliefs with some person at the top yapping about some mission that we're on and genuinely... deep belief that that's real." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner] Strong reasons are what make humans endure risk — his historical analogy is settlers crossing the Atlantic and the Rockies.

**Principle: "Demonize the corpo."** Prematurely importing corporate systems (he names EOS) at a couple-hundred-K business is a facade that distracts from the real bottleneck in the trenches (e.g., not enough qualified calls, not enough salespeople). "You got to get bigger before you do that kind of [ __ ]." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner] Stay "extremely tapped in to whatever is holding you back in the trenches," and don't make the problem sound more complicated than it is.

**On "killers" / "sickos" — effortless-looking high achievers.** He admires people who are simply "dialed the [ __ ] in" (his friend Judge Graham; Shohei Ohtani) and argues their ease comes from hyper-intentional obsessive reps, not from grinding visibly. "a lot of people aren't intentional when they... do the work," whereas the obsessed are "highly intentional with what they are doing." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means]

## 8. Decision-making: don't compromise the original vision; invest past your comfort

**Principle: When priced out or blocked, get bigger than the decision instead of compromising — compromises compound into a life you never chose.**

His land story: priced out of 5 acres in Miami (jumped from $500K to $3M after rezoning), he compromised and bought 15.5 acres in Texas, then felt the wrongness of it and refused to use it for the original purpose. The lesson:

- "I'm not going to compromise what I actually want in my journey of getting richer." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]
- "why not instead just say how can I get bigger than the decision I need to make so I can actually make it." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]
- On loss aversion when "too many decisions deep": "shut up get back to the root drive that you initially set out to accomplish and make real." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

**Principle: Spending serious money is what forces you to take things seriously — invest more than is comfortable.** He is explicit that this is partly a pitch for his own programs, but presents it as a genuine mechanism.

- "What you spend a good amount of money on is what you'll take most seriously." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]
- "invest a fuckload more money than you make right now. A fuckload more than you're comfortable with... Because you have to." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]

**Principle: Welcome extreme accountability and never stop consuming new information.** Big earners love being pushed; complacency comes from two sources — no new information and no one holding you accountable.

- "There is an extreme level of accountability that all these big dogs welcome into their lives. They [ __ ] love accountability." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]
- "You have to consume new information to think with new options." [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]

**On learning the hard way vs. seeking the knowledge.** His $37K loss on a non-warranty Rolls (bought without a pre-purchase inspection) is his cautionary tale: "it's a fool's game to learn the hard way." [The Honest Truth About Money, Freedom & What Being Rich Really Means] Resourcefulness closes the gap — information like his is freely available: "All you have to do is be resourceful." [Rewire Yourself To Think Like Top 0.1% Entrepreneur In 26 Mins]

**Guard against ambiguity by simplifying to the one real bottleneck.** Across the live-consult patterns, his decision method under "what do I do next?" ambiguity is: strip away the elaborate narrative, find the single constraint in the trenches, and bias toward aggressive action on it. Don't ask for one marketing metric — go get more qualified calls and hire more salespeople. [Give Me 24 Mins… And I'll Show You How To Think Like A $1M/Month Earner]

## 9. Money, freedom, identity, and the long game

**Principle: You are in accumulation mode, racing a closing earning window, and almost nobody does the math on what their life actually costs.**

- "Today's dollars matter the most to invest." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "everybody has an earning window... a period of time where your current skills are the most valuable they will be in your life." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]

He uses his own math: the 4% rule, the investor.gov compound interest calculator, discovering at 24 he'd need ~$100M+ in equities and his first seven-figure tax bill — to argue people drastically undervalue how much they need and therefore "stay smaller than you should be." **Principle: Rich people carry a responsibility, and refusing to keep getting richer is selfish.** He argues the rich can "materialize better conditions for the planet" with speed, and that stopping at personal comfort means you've only taken care of yourself.

- "there's nothing more selfish than being rich and only supporting you." ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]

## 10. Self-actualization: meet the better you by building him daily

**Principle: Your growth target is the fullest version of yourself; becoming him is a daily, repetitive, honest-effort practice — and it's enjoyable, not grim.**

He cites Maslow's hierarchy and Ed Mylett's "deathbed / meet your fullest potential self" idea. The frame is both motivating and happy:

- "God gave you a mission that is greater than your current self can handle." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- "To be a better self tomorrow, you must build the better self every day." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]
- The antidote to the "I'll be better tomorrow" plague of regular men: judge reality today, try hard, be consistent — "that anger, just a version of you inside of yourself begging to come out." [A Brutally Honest Talk On Why You Need To Get A F**kload Richer]

He also insists this is individual — there's no single motivational message; each person must find and get genuinely excited about their own driver (generational wealth, charitable impact, or proving expanded potential), then exponentially (not incrementally) grow from where they are. ["Get Richer" - WATCH THIS IF YOU LOST YOUR EDGE]
